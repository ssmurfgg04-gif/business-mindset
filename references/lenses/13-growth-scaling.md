# Lens 13: Growth & Scaling — How Do I Grow This Effectively?

<lens>
<core_question>
At this stage of the business, what is the single highest-leverage growth motion —
and what should I explicitly NOT do yet?
</core_question>

<when_to_use>
- User asks "how do I grow?" or "how do I scale?"
- After product-market fit is confirmed (or to test if it is)
- When growth has plateaued
- When transitioning from one stage to another (0-1M ARR → 1-10M, etc.)
- When evaluating distribution channel options
- When user is considering hiring a growth/marketing person
</when_to_use>

<when_not_to_use>
- Pre-PMF (use Lens 01-05 to find PMF first; growth pre-PMF = premature scaling)
- Single opportunity evaluation (growth is downstream of opportunity selection)
</when_not_to_use>
</lens>

## Core Philosophy

<principle>
Growth is stage-dependent. The motion that takes you from 0 to $1M ARR is
different from what takes you from $1M to $10M, which is different from $10M
to $100M. The single most common growth mistake is applying the wrong motion
to the wrong stage — typically, scaling sales and marketing before PMF
(premature scaling, responsible for 74% of startup failures per CB Insights).

This lens identifies the current stage, prescribes the primary growth motion
for that stage, and explicitly lists what NOT to do yet. The "NOT yet" list
is more important than the "do" list — premature scaling kills more businesses
than under-scaling.
</principle>

## The 4 Growth Stages

<growth_stages>

### Stage 0: Pre-Product-Market Fit (0 → $10K MRR)

<stage_0>
**Primary motion**: Customer development. Find the segment + use case + value
prop combination that retains customers without you pushing them.

**Key metrics to track**:
- **Sean Ellis test**: ≥40% of customers would be "very disappointed" without your product. <40% = no PMF.
- **Retention curve**: Does it flatten (PMF) or continue declining (no PMF)?
- **Organic acquisition**: Are customers finding you without paid spend? (Strong PMF signal)
- **Word of mouth**: NPS >40, unsolicited referrals

**What to do**:
- Talk to 10-20 customers/month. Understand their actual job (Christensen JTBD).
- Iterate on the product weekly based on customer feedback.
- Charge from day 1 (free users don't give honest feedback).
- Manual sales (founder-led). Don't hire sales yet.
- Manual onboarding. Don't build self-serve yet.

**What NOT to do yet**:
- Don't hire sales. Founder sells until you can articulate the pitch in your sleep.
- Don't hire marketing. You don't know what to say yet.
- Don't run paid acquisition. CAC will be catastrophic without PMF.
- Don't build growth features (referrals, viral loops). You don't have the user base.
- Don't scale operations. Stay manual.

**Common failure mode**: Premature scaling. Founder hires sales/marketing before
PMF, burns capital, no growth. CB Insights: 74% of failures.

**Stage exit criteria** (graduate to Stage 1):
- $10K+ MRR
- Sean Ellis test ≥40%
- Retention curve flattens
- 3+ months of <5% monthly churn
- Clear articulation of ICP (ideal customer profile)
</stage_0>

### Stage 1: Early PMF ($10K → $1M MRR)

<stage_1>
**Primary motion**: Double down on what's working. Find the ONE channel that
drives most growth and pour resources into it. Resist the temptation to
diversify channels prematurely.

**Key metrics**:
- **CAC payback**: <12 months (SaaS benchmark). <6 months is excellent.
- **LTV:CAC**: ≥3:1. <3:1 = channel doesn't work. >5:1 = under-investing.
- **NRR**: ≥100% (existing customers don't shrink). 110%+ is excellent.
- **Monthly growth rate**: 10-20% (early stage). Compounds fast.

**What to do**:
- Identify the dominant channel (from Lens 05 analysis). Could be SEO, content, outbound, partnerships, community, paid.
- Hire ONE person for that channel (not a "growth team" — one specialist).
- Build the playbook for that channel. Document everything.
- Founder still does enterprise sales personally. Don't delegate yet.
- Start tracking cohorts. Retention by cohort, channel, segment.
- Raise prices annually (Lens 09).

**What NOT to do yet**:
- Don't diversify channels. One channel at >3:1 LTV:CAC beats five channels at 1:1.
- Don't hire a VP of Sales. You're still figuring out the sales motion.
- Don't build a "growth team." That's a Stage 2 luxury.
- Don't raise a big round. $1-3M max if needed. Stay capital-efficient.
- Don't expand to new segments. Nail the current ICP first.

**Common failure mode**: Channel diversification. Founder tries SEO + paid +
outbound + partnerships simultaneously, does none well, burns runway.

**Stage exit criteria**:
- $1M+ ARR
- One channel with proven 3:1+ LTV:CAC
- CAC payback <12 months
- NRR ≥100%
- 6+ months of consistent growth
</stage_1>

### Stage 2: Scaling ($1M → $10M MRR)

<stage_2>
**Primary motion**: Replicate the proven motion. Scale the channel that worked
in Stage 1. Add a second channel only when the first is saturated.

**Key metrics**:
- **Magic Number**: ≥0.5 (good), ≥1.0 (excellent). Formula: (Quarterly ARR growth) / (S&M spend previous quarter).
- **Rule of 40**: Growth rate % + profit margin % ≥ 40. (e.g., 50% growth + -10% margin = 40).
- **NRR**: ≥110% (expansion revenue exceeds churn).
- **Gross margin**: ≥70% (SaaS). <70% = either pricing or cost problem.
- **Burn multiple**: <1.5 (good), <1.0 (excellent). Formula: Net burn / net new ARR.

**What to do**:
- Hire VP of Sales (if sales-led) or VP of Growth (if product-led).
- Build the sales playbook. Replicate founder's sales process.
- Add second channel (after first is saturated). Common pairs: SEO + paid, content + outbound, PLG + sales-assist.
- Hire customer success team. NRR becomes critical.
- Build self-serve onboarding (if PLG) or sales-assisted funnel (if SLG).
- Start international expansion (if applicable).

**What NOT to do yet**:
- Don't build a third channel. Two channels is plenty. Three = dilution.
- Don't launch multiple products. Stay focused on the core.
- Don't hire a "Chief Strategy Officer" or build a strategy team. Execute.
- Don't raise at crazy valuations. Capital efficiency compounds.

**Common failure mode**: Over-hiring. Founder hires 20 people in 6 months,
culture breaks, productivity per employee drops, runway burns. Hire 1-2/month max.

**Stage exit criteria**:
- $10M+ ARR
- Two channels with proven unit economics
- Magic Number ≥0.5
- Rule of 40 ≥ 40
- Team of 30-80 people, culture intact
</stage_2>

### Stage 3: Scale ($10M → $100M MRR)

<stage_3>
**Primary motion**: Multi-channel scaling + product expansion. Now you diversify.

**Key metrics**:
- **Magic Number**: ≥0.7 (mature SaaS)
- **Rule of 40**: ≥40 (now more focused on profitability)
- **NRR**: ≥115%
- **Gross margin**: ≥75%
- **Customer concentration**: No single customer >5% of revenue

**What to do**:
- Add 3rd and 4th channels (events, ABM, partnerships, international).
- Launch adjacent products (line extensions).
- Build a real executive team (CRO, CMO, CPO, CFO).
- Geographic expansion (US → EU → APAC).
- Consider M&A (acquiring competitors or adjacent products).

**What NOT to do yet**:
- Don't go public prematurely. IPO readiness = 2-3 years of prep.
- Don't lose founder mentality. "Day 2" culture (Bezos) = death.

**Common failure mode**: Becoming an "enterprise" with enterprise dysfunction.
Process bloat, decision slowness, customer distance. Bezos's Day 1 principle
matters most at this stage.

**Stage exit criteria**:
- $100M+ ARR
- Multiple products, multiple geographies, multiple channels
- Clear IPO or strategic exit path
</stage_3>
</growth_stages>

## Distribution Channel Deep-Dive

<channels>
For each major channel: when it works, CAC ranges, time-to-results, key tactics, common mistakes.

### SEO
- **When works**: High-intent queries ("[product] alternative", "how to [job]")
- **CAC**: $50-500 (depends on competition)
- **Time-to-results**: 6-18 months (slow, then compounds)
- **Key tactics**: Topic clusters, backlink building, technical SEO, content velocity
- **Common mistakes**: Targeting head terms (too competitive), ignoring technical SEO, inconsistent publishing

### Content marketing
- **When works**: B2B considered purchase, education-led sales
- **CAC**: $100-1000
- **Time-to-results**: 6-12 months
- **Key tactics**: Pillar content, distribution > creation, repurpose across channels
- **Common mistakes**: Publishing without distribution, no CTA, no measurement

### Paid acquisition (Google Ads)
- **When works**: High-intent search, clear value prop, strong landing page
- **CAC**: $100-2000 (depends on industry)
- **Time-to-results**: 1-2 weeks (immediate, then optimize)
- **Key tactics**: Single keyword ad groups, negative keywords, landing page testing
- **Common mistakes**: Broad match, no conversion tracking, no landing page testing

### Paid acquisition (Meta/TikTok)
- **When works**: B2C, impulse purchase, visual products
- **CAC**: $20-300
- **Time-to-results**: 2-4 weeks
- **Key tactics**: Creative testing (3+ per week), lookalike audiences, retargeting
- **Common mistakes**: One creative, no retargeting, broad audience

### Outbound sales
- **When works**: B2B, high ACV (>$10K/yr), defined ICP
- **CAC**: $500-5000
- **Time-to-results**: 1-3 months (long sales cycles)
- **Key tactics**: Personalized outreach (not templates), multi-touch (email + LinkedIn + call), follow-up
- **Common mistakes**: Generic templates, no follow-up, no ICP definition
- **Reply rate benchmarks**: Cold email 3-5% (good), 5-10% (excellent). LinkedIn 10-20%.

### Partnerships
- **When works**: Complementary products, shared ICP
- **CAC**: $50-500 (lower because shared)
- **Time-to-results**: 3-6 months (relationship building)
- **Key tactics**: Co-marketing, integration partnerships, reseller agreements
- **Common mistakes**: One-sided partnerships, no clear value to partner, no measurement

### Influencer marketing
- **When works**: B2C, visual products, niche audiences
- **CAC**: $50-1000
- **Time-to-results**: 1-2 months
- **Key tactics**: Micro-influencers (1K-100K followers), affiliate model (pay per conversion), authentic content
- **Common mistakes**: Mega-influencers (low engagement), pay-per-post (no incentive for conversion), no tracking

### Community-led growth
- **When works**: Developer tools, technical products, niche professional communities
- **CAC**: $20-200 (low because community attracts)
- **Time-to-results**: 6-18 months (slow build)
- **Key tactics**: Discord/Slack community, open-source (if applicable), events, content
- **Common mistakes**: Treating community as marketing channel (it's a product), no community manager, no value to free members

### Product-led growth (PLG)
- **When works**: Self-serve product, clear aha moment, viral or sticky mechanics
- **CAC**: $20-200 (low because product acquires)
- **Time-to-results**: 3-6 months to see flywheel
- **Key tactics**: Free tier or trial, in-product onboarding, usage-based pricing, viral loops
- **Common mistakes**: Free tier too generous (no conversion), no onboarding (drop-off), no expansion (no upsell)

### Sales-led growth (SLG)
- **When works**: High ACV (>$50K/yr), complex product, enterprise buyers
- **CAC**: $5K-50K (high but justified by ACV)
- **Time-to-results**: 3-9 months (long sales cycles)
- **Key tactics**: SDR → AE → CSM structure, account-based marketing (ABM), demo-led sales
- **Common mistakes**: Hiring SDRs too early (before AE playbook), no ICP definition, no sales ops
</channels>

## Metrics Reference

<metrics>
| Metric | Formula | Benchmark (SaaS) | When it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sean Ellis test** | % "very disappointed" without product | ≥40% = PMF | Pre-PMF |
| **NRR** | (Starting MRR + expansion - churn - downgrade) / Starting MRR | ≥110% enterprise, ≥100% SMB | Stage 1+ |
| **GRR** | (Starting MRR - churn - downgrade) / Starting MRR | ≥90% | Stage 1+ |
| **LTV:CAC** | LTV / CAC | ≥3:1 | Stage 1+ |
| **CAC payback** | CAC / (monthly gross margin per customer) | <12 months | Stage 1+ |
| **Magic Number** | (Quarterly ARR growth × 4) / (prior quarter S&M spend) | ≥0.5 good, ≥1.0 excellent | Stage 2+ |
| **Rule of 40** | Growth rate % + profit margin % | ≥40 | Stage 2+ |
| **Burn multiple** | Net burn / net new ARR | <1.5 good, <1.0 excellent | Stage 1+ |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | ≥70% SaaS | All stages |
| **Monthly churn** | (Customers lost / starting customers) | <5% SMB, <2% enterprise | All stages |
| **NPS** | % promoters - % detractors | ≥40 good, ≥50 excellent | All stages |
| **Cohort retention** | % of cohort still active at month N | Flattens = PMF | Pre-PMF, ongoing |
</metrics>

## Few-Shot Examples

<examples>

### Example 1: Pre-PMF SaaS (positive)

<example>
<situation>
SaaS product at $5K MRR, 8% monthly churn, founder doing all sales. Asks "how do I scale?"
</situation>

<thinking>
Let me check the stage.
- MRR: $5K → Stage 0 (pre-PMF, since $10K is the threshold)
- Churn: 8% monthly → WAY too high. Should be <5% SMB, <2% enterprise.
- Sean Ellis test: not mentioned, but 8% churn strongly suggests <40% (no PMF)

This is pre-PMF. The user is asking about scaling, but they don't have PMF yet. Scaling pre-PMF = premature scaling = 74% failure cause.

The recommendation is NOT to scale. The recommendation is to find PMF.

What does finding PMF look like?
1. Talk to churned customers. Why did they leave? Common pattern = the problem to fix.
2. Talk to retained customers. Why do they stay? This is your real value prop.
3. Run Sean Ellis test. If <40%, no PMF.
4. Iterate product based on what retained customers value.
5. Don't spend on marketing. Don't hire sales. Don't run paid acquisition.

The 8% monthly churn is the loudest signal. That's ~60% annual churn. The product is leaking users faster than acquisition can replace them. No amount of growth marketing fixes a leaky bucket.

Specific actions:
- Stop all acquisition spend (if any)
- Interview 10 churned customers this week
- Interview 10 retained customers this week
- Run Sean Ellis test with all current customers
- Identify the segment with <3% monthly churn (your real ICP)
- Pivot positioning to that segment
- Rebuild onboarding for that segment
- Once churn <5% and Sean Ellis ≥40%, THEN think about growth
</thinking>

<answer>
**You're pre-PMF. Do not scale.**

Stage 0 signals:
- $5K MRR (below $10K threshold)
- 8% monthly churn (WAY above 5% benchmark — you're leaking 60% annually)
- No Sean Ellis test mentioned (likely <40% = no PMF)

**The problem isn't growth. The problem is retention.** No amount of acquisition fixes a leaky bucket. 8% monthly churn means you need 96% annual growth just to break even.

**What to do (NOT scaling)**:
1. Stop all acquisition spend
2. Interview 10 churned customers — why did they leave?
3. Interview 10 retained customers — why do they stay?
4. Run Sean Ellis test (≥40% "very disappointed" = PMF)
5. Identify the segment with <3% monthly churn (your real ICP)
6. Pivot positioning and onboarding to that segment
7. Iterate until: churn <5%, Sean Ellis ≥40%, retention curve flattens

**What NOT to do**:
- Don't hire sales (founder still sells)
- Don't run paid acquisition (CAC will be catastrophic)
- Don't hire marketing (you don't know what to say)
- Don't build growth features (no user base to amplify)
- Don't raise money (you'll just burn it on premature scaling)

**Exit criteria for Stage 0** (when you can think about growth):
- $10K+ MRR
- Churn <5% monthly
- Sean Ellis ≥40%
- Retention curve flattens
- Clear ICP articulation

Then come back and ask about growth. Right now, the answer is: fix the product.
</answer>
</example>

## Edge Cases

<edge_cases>
### Edge 1: Viral growth (rare)
Some products grow virally (Slack, Calendly). Viral coefficient >1 means each
user brings >1 new user. If you have this, the rules change — pour fuel on the
fire, don't worry about channel diversification. But verify the viral coefficient
is real, not a temporary spike.

### Edge 2: Enterprise-only business
If ACV is $100K+, you don't need 1000 customers. You need 50. The growth motion
is outbound + ABM, not SEO or PLG. Don't apply SMB growth advice to enterprise.

### Edge 3: Marketplace
Two-sided growth is different. You must solve the chicken-and-egg problem first
(subsidize one side), then growth compounds. Don't apply single-sided growth
frameworks to marketplaces.

### Edge 4: Hardware / physical product
Growth is constrained by supply chain, inventory, fulfillment. The growth motion
includes operational scaling (manufacturing, warehousing, shipping) that software
businesses don't face. Don't apply software growth advice to hardware.

### Edge 5: Plateaued growth
If growth plateaus at Stage 1 or 2, the cause is usually:
- Channel saturation (need to add second channel)
- Pricing too low (need to raise prices — Lens 09)
- Wrong ICP (need to re-segment)
- Product not improving (need to ship features)
Diagnose before treating. Don't just "do more marketing."

### Edge 6: Hyper-growth (>$10M ARR growing >100% YoY)
Rare. Requires exceptional execution. The risk shifts from "not enough growth"
to "operations can't keep up." Hire ahead of need, build processes before you
need them, watch for culture degradation.
</edge_cases>

## Decision Protocol

<decision_protocol>
### Exact Question This Lens Answers
"At this stage, what is the single highest-leverage growth motion, and what
should I NOT do yet?"

### Data Required
- Current MRR/ARR
- Monthly churn rate (gross and net)
- NRR (net revenue retention)
- LTV:CAC ratio (by channel)
- CAC payback period
- Sean Ellis test result (if pre-PMF)
- Current channels and their unit economics
- Cohort retention data

### Confidence Threshold
- **Deploy (execute growth motion)**: ≥80% confidence, stage clearly identified, metrics support the motion
- **Flag (proceed with caution)**: 60-80% confidence, stage unclear, some metrics ambiguous
- **Discard (don't scale yet)**: <60% confidence, OR pre-PMF signals present (high churn, low Sean Ellis)

### Conflict Resolution Rules
- When Lens 13 (Growth) disagrees with user desire to scale:
  - If pre-PMF: growth lens wins. Don't scale pre-PMF.
  - If stage unclear: run the diagnostic, then decide.
- When Lens 13 disagrees with Lens 12 (Operations):
  - Operations may need to scale ahead of growth (hire ops person before growth spikes)
- When Lens 13 disagrees with Lens 09 (Pricing):
  - Sometimes raising prices (Lens 09) is the highest-leverage growth motion. Don't add channels when pricing is broken.
- When Lens 13 disagrees with Lens 08 (Risk of Ruin):
  - Risk of ruin wins. Don't scale if scaling risks bankruptcy.
</decision_protocol>
