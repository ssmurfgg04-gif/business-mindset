# Lens 17: Intuition & Pattern Recognition — When to Trust Your Gut

<lens>
<core_question>
What does my gut say, is my gut calibrated enough to trust, and when should
intuition override analysis (or vice versa)?
</core_question>

<when_to_use>
- When analysis is incomplete or slow (intuition is faster)
- When the decision involves pattern recognition from deep experience
- When something feels "off" but you can't articulate why
- When evaluating people (hiring, partnerships) — reading people is intuitive
- When assessing product taste / aesthetic judgment
- When the opportunity requires contrarian conviction (Lens 18)
- After all rational analysis is complete, as a final check
</when_to_use>

<when_not_to_use>
- When you have no domain expertise (amateur intuition = bias, not pattern recognition)
- When the decision is irreversible and high-stakes (analysis should dominate)
- When your gut is emotionally driven (fear, excitement) rather than pattern-driven
- As a substitute for verification (intuition is a hypothesis, not a conclusion)
</when_not_to_use>
</lens>

## Core Philosophy

<principle>
**The skill has been hyper-rational up to this point. That's a flaw.**

The greatest operators — Jobs, Musk, Buffett, Munger, Bezos, Walton — all
report making major decisions on intuition that looked irrational at the time.
Jobs killed the iPod mini when it was 50% of Apple's revenue (intuition said
iPhone would cannibalize it anyway). Musk committed his last $35M to Tesla
in 2008 (intuition said the alternative was worse). Buffett bought American
Express during the salad oil scandal (intuition said the brand would survive).

**The insight**: Intuition is NOT anti-rational. It's **compressed pattern
recognition**. After 10,000+ hours in a domain, the brain recognizes patterns
faster than conscious analysis can articulate them. Kahneman calls this
"System 1 expert intuition" — distinct from amateur intuition (which is just
bias).

**The rule**: Expert intuition is a legitimate signal. Amateur intuition is
bias. The skill must distinguish between them.
</principle>

## The 2 Types of Intuition

<two_types>

### Type 1: Expert Intuition (calibrated pattern recognition)
**What**: Pattern recognition built from 10,000+ hours of deep domain experience.
The expert "just knows" without being able to fully articulate why.

**Examples**:
- Chess grandmaster sees the right move in 5 seconds (10,000+ hours of study)
- Senior engineer smells a bad architecture before analyzing it
- Experienced founder reads a pitch and "knows" it won't work
- Buffett reads a 10-K and senses something off

**Why it works**: The brain has built a pattern library from thousands of
cases. New situations match stored patterns faster than conscious analysis.

**When to trust**: When you have 10,000+ hours in the domain AND the current
situation matches patterns you've seen before.

**When NOT to trust**: In novel situations with no precedent (your pattern
library doesn't apply), or in domains where you lack expertise.

### Type 2: Amateur Intuition (bias dressed as feeling)
**What**: Emotional responses (fear, excitement, ego) that FEEL like intuition
but are actually cognitive biases.

**Examples**:
- "I have a good feeling about this" (no domain expertise — just optimism bias)
- "My gut says this will work" (after reading one article — availability bias)
- "I just know they're the right hire" (after one interview — halo effect)
- "This feels wrong" (without being able to articulate why — loss aversion)

**Why it fails**: No pattern library. The "feeling" is emotion, not recognition.

**When to NEVER trust**: When you lack domain expertise, when the feeling is
emotional rather than recognition-based, when you can't articulate the pattern.

**The test**: Can you articulate WHY your gut says this? If yes (even
imperfectly), it's expert intuition. If no ("I just have a feeling"), it's
amateur intuition — ignore it.
</two_types>

## The Intuition Calibration Framework

<calibration>
Intuition is only as good as its calibration. An uncalibrated gut is dangerous.
A calibrated gut is a competitive advantage.

### How to calibrate your gut

#### 1. Track your gut calls
For every significant intuition-driven decision, record:
- What did your gut say? (specific prediction)
- How confident did you feel? (0-100%)
- What did analysis say? (agree or disagree with gut?)
- What did you decide? (followed gut or analysis?)
- What was the outcome? (success/failure, recorded later)

Store in decision journal (`references/decision-journal.md`) with `gut_call`
field.

#### 2. Compute your gut accuracy
After 20+ tracked gut calls:
- Of gut calls you followed, what % succeeded? (gut precision)
- Of gut calls you ignored, what % would have succeeded? (gut recall)
- In domains where you have 10,000+ hours, is gut more accurate than analysis?
- In domains where you lack expertise, is gut less accurate? (should be)

#### 3. Identify your calibrated domains
You'll find your gut is calibrated in some domains (your expertise) and not
others. Map:
- **Calibrated domains** (gut > analysis): trust intuition here
- **Uncalibrated domains** (gut = random): always require analysis
- **Anti-calibrated domains** (gut < analysis): invert your gut (do the opposite)

#### 4. Update calibration over time
Gut calibration drifts. Re-compute annually. Domains that were calibrated can
become uncalibrated (markets change, your knowledge becomes stale). New domains
can become calibrated (you gain expertise).

### The calibration verdict

| Gut accuracy (in domain) | Verdict | Action |
|--------------------------|---------|--------|
| >70% | Well-calibrated | Trust gut; analysis as check |
| 50-70% | Marginal | Use gut as one signal, not decisive |
| <50% | Uncalibrated | Ignore gut; analysis only |
| <30% (over time) | Anti-calibrated | Invert gut; do the opposite |
</calibration>

## The 5 Intuition Signals

<signals>
These are the specific "gut feelings" that expert operators report. Each has
a name, a description, and how to validate it.

### Signal 1: The "Something's Off" Feeling
**What**: A vague sense that something is wrong, even though analysis looks fine.

**Expert operators report**: "I couldn't put my finger on it, but something
felt off about the deal."

**How to validate**:
- Ask: "What specific pattern does this remind me of?" (force articulation)
- Ask: "What would have to be true for this feeling to be correct?"
- If you can articulate the pattern → expert intuition. Investigate.
- If you can't articulate → amateur intuition. Set aside (but don't ignore —
  revisit after more analysis).

**Example**: Buffett reading a 10-K and sensing accounting irregularities
before the fraud was exposed. Pattern recognition from thousands of 10-Ks.

### Signal 2: The "This Is The One" Feeling
**What**: A strong sense that this opportunity/person/product is special,
distinct from general enthusiasm.

**Expert operators report**: "When I saw [X], I just knew."

**How to validate**:
- Ask: "What specific pattern am I recognizing?" (the markers that make this
  special)
- Ask: "How many times have I felt this before? What was the outcome?"
- If you've felt this before AND been right → strong signal
- If this is the first time → weak signal; validate with analysis

**Example**: Jobs seeing the GUI at Xerox PARC and knowing it was the future.
Pattern recognition from years of thinking about human-computer interaction.

### Signal 3: The "This Won't Work" Feeling
**What**: Immediate skepticism that resists persuasion, even when the pitch
sounds good.

**Expert operators report**: "Everyone was excited, but I couldn't shake the
feeling it wouldn't work."

**How to validate**:
- Ask: "What's the specific failure mode I'm sensing?"
- Ask: "Does this remind me of past failures? Which ones?"
- If you can name the failure mode → expert intuition. Investigate.
- If it's just "bad vibes" → amateur intuition. Set aside.

**Example**: Munger passing on investments that "looked good on paper" because
he sensed something off. Often correct.

### Signal 4: The "Move Now" Feeling
**What**: Urgency that this window is closing, that waiting is more dangerous
than acting.

**Expert operators report**: "I knew we had to move immediately."

**How to validate**:
- Ask: "What specific signal indicates the window is closing?"
- Ask: "Is this FOMO (emotion) or pattern recognition (expertise)?"
- If you can identify the closing signal → expert intuition. Act.
- If it's just "I'll miss out" → FOMO. Slow down.

**Example**: Musk committing to Tesla in 2008. Pattern recognition that the
window for EV was opening and capital was drying up.

### Signal 5: The "Wait" Feeling
**What**: Sense that acting now is premature, even though external pressure
says move.

**Expert operators report**: "Everyone wanted me to decide, but I knew I
should wait."

**How to validate**:
- Ask: "What am I waiting for? What signal would tell me to act?"
- Ask: "Is this patience (expertise) or avoidance (fear)?"
- If you can name the signal you're waiting for → expert intuition. Wait.
- If it's just "I don't want to decide" → avoidance. Decide.

**Example**: Buffett waiting for the right pitch. "You don't have to swing
at every ball."
</signals>

## The Gut-Analysis Integration Framework

<integration>
The skill doesn't replace analysis with intuition. It integrates them.

### The 4 integration patterns:

#### Pattern 1: Analysis-First, Gut-Check
**When**: Most decisions (default mode)
**Process**:
1. Run full analysis (Lenses 01-16)
2. Ask: "Does my gut agree with the analysis?"
3. If YES → high confidence in verdict
4. If NO → investigate the disagreement:
   - Can you articulate why your gut disagrees?
   - Is your gut calibrated in this domain?
   - What would have to be true for your gut to be right?
5. If gut disagreement is articulable and calibrated → override analysis
6. If gut disagreement is vague or uncalibrated → trust analysis, note the
   gut feeling for later

#### Pattern 2: Gut-First, Analysis-Validate
**When**: You have deep expertise AND the decision is time-sensitive
**Process**:
1. Note your gut reaction immediately
2. Articulate the pattern you're recognizing
3. Run targeted analysis to validate/refute the gut
4. If analysis confirms → act on gut (faster)
5. If analysis refutes → trust analysis, understand why gut was wrong
6. If analysis is inconclusive → gut is tiebreaker IF calibrated

#### Pattern 3: Gut Veto
**When**: Analysis says PASS but gut says NO
**Process**:
1. Don't override PASS without understanding the gut veto
2. Ask: "What is my gut sensing that analysis missed?"
3. Investigate specifically for that failure mode
4. If you find evidence → downgrade PASS to FLAG
5. If you find no evidence → trust analysis, but note the gut veto
6. Track outcome: was gut right? (feeds calibration)

#### Pattern 4: Analysis Veto
**When**: Gut says GO but analysis says STOP
**Process**:
1. Always trust analysis over uncalibrated gut
2. Ask: "Is my gut calibrated in this domain?"
3. If yes → investigate why analysis and gut disagree
4. If no → trust analysis. Your gut is bias.
5. NEVER override analysis with amateur intuition.

### The integration rule
**For high-stakes decisions, require BOTH gut and analysis to agree.** If
they disagree, investigate the disagreement before deciding. Don't suppress
either — both are signals.
</integration>

## The Domain Expertise Audit

<expertise_audit>
Before trusting intuition, audit your actual domain expertise.

### The 10,000-hour test
- Have you spent 10,000+ hours in this domain? (5+ years full-time)
- Have you made 100+ decisions in this domain?
- Have you seen both successes and failures in this domain?
- Can you articulate the key patterns and anti-patterns?

If YES to all → expert intuition applicable
If NO to any → amateur intuition; require analysis

### The track record test
- Of past gut calls in this domain, what % were correct?
- Do you have 20+ tracked gut calls to compute accuracy?
- Is your gut more accurate than analysis in this domain?

If YES → trust gut
If NO → trust analysis

### The articulation test
- Can you articulate WHY your gut says this?
- Can you name the pattern you're recognizing?
- Can you identify what evidence would change your gut?

If YES → expert intuition
If NO → amateur intuition (bias)
</expertise_audit>

## Few-Shot Examples

<examples>

### Example 1: Expert intuition (positive)

<example>
<situation>
Senior engineer (15 years experience) reviews a startup's technical architecture. Analysis says it's viable. Gut says "this won't scale."
</situation>

<thinking>
Let me run the intuition lens.

Domain expertise audit:
- 15 years experience = ~30,000 hours ✓ (passes 10,000-hour test)
- 100+ architecture decisions ✓
- Has seen scaling failures ✓
- Can articulate patterns ✓

This is expert intuition, not amateur.

The "This Won't Work" feeling. Let me validate:
- Can I articulate the failure mode? Yes — the database choice won't handle
  the read pattern they're describing. I've seen this fail at [previous company].
- Does this remind me of past failures? Yes — [specific past failure].
- Is this pattern recognition or skepticism? Pattern recognition — I can name
  the specific issue.

Calibration: my gut on architecture has been right ~80% of the time over 15
years. This is a calibrated domain.

Integration pattern: Gut Veto. Analysis says PASS (architecture is technically
viable). Gut says NO (won't scale). Investigate the specific failure mode.

The investigation: look at the database choice, the read patterns, the
expected scale. If my gut is right, there's a specific bottleneck the analysis
missed.
</thinking>

<answer>
**Gut signal**: "This won't scale" — expert intuition (15 years, 30K hours,
calibrated 80% accuracy)

**Validation**: Can articulate the failure mode (database won't handle read
pattern). Reminds me of [specific past failure]. This is pattern recognition,
not bias.

**Integration**: Gut Veto pattern. Analysis says PASS, gut says NO. Investigate
the specific scaling concern before deciding.

**Action**: Don't override the PASS yet — but investigate the database scaling
specifically. If the investigation confirms the gut, downgrade to FLAG. If
not, trust analysis.

**The insight**: The gut is sensing a specific failure mode the analysis
didn't check. That's the value of expert intuition — it directs attention
to what analysis missed.
</answer>
</example>

### Example 2: Amateur intuition (negative)

<example>
<situation>
First-time founder with no healthcare experience "has a good feeling" about building a healthcare app. Analysis shows regulatory barriers.
</situation>

<thinking>
Domain expertise audit:
- 0 hours in healthcare ✗ (fails 10,000-hour test)
- 0 healthcare decisions ✗
- Hasn't seen healthcare successes/failures ✗
- Can't articulate healthcare patterns ✗

This is amateur intuition. The "good feeling" is optimism bias, not pattern
recognition.

The articulation test: can they articulate WHY their gut says this? "It just
feels like a good opportunity" = no articulation = amateur.

Integration pattern: Analysis Veto. Gut says GO, analysis says STOP (regulatory
barriers). Trust analysis. The gut is bias.
</thinking>

<answer>
**Gut signal**: "Good feeling" — amateur intuition (0 hours in healthcare,
no track record, can't articulate)

**Validation**: Cannot articulate why. No domain expertise. This is optimism
bias, not pattern recognition.

**Integration**: Analysis Veto pattern. Analysis says regulatory barriers are
fatal. Gut says GO. Trust analysis. The gut is bias.

**Action**: REJECT. Do not pursue healthcare opportunity without domain
expertise. The "good feeling" is not a signal — it's bias.

**The insight**: First-time founders often mistake enthusiasm for intuition.
Enthusiasm without expertise is bias. The skill must distinguish them.
</answer>
</example>
</examples>

## Decision Protocol

<decision_protocol>
### Exact Question
"What does my gut say, is it calibrated enough to trust, and when should
intuition override analysis?"

### Data Required
- Domain expertise audit (10,000 hours? 100+ decisions? track record?)
- Gut articulation (can you name the pattern?)
- Calibration data (historical gut accuracy in this domain)
- Analysis verdict (does gut agree or disagree?)

### Confidence Threshold
- **Trust gut (override analysis)**: ≥80% gut accuracy in domain, articulable
  pattern, calibrated
- **Use gut as one signal**: 50-80% accuracy, partially articulable
- **Ignore gut (trust analysis)**: <50% accuracy OR amateur intuition OR
  can't articulate

### Conflict Resolution
- Gut + analysis agree → high confidence, proceed
- Gut says GO, analysis says STOP → trust analysis (amateur gut is bias)
- Analysis says GO, gut says STOP → investigate (expert gut may have caught
  what analysis missed)
- If gut and analysis persistently disagree → don't decide. Get more information.
</decision_protocol>
