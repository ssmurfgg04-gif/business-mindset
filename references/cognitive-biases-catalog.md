# Cognitive Biases Catalog — 30 Biases That Distort Business Decisions

A reference catalog of the cognitive biases most likely to distort business
decisions, organized by category. Each bias: what it is, how it manifests
in business, and the counter-tactic.

Based on Kahneman & Tversky's prospect theory, Munger's "Psychology of Human
Misjudgment" (25 biases), and the broader cognitive psychology literature.

## Why This Matters

<principle>
"You can't avoid biases. But you can build systems that counteract them."
— Kahneman

Most bias training fails because it tries to eliminate biases through
awareness. That doesn't work — biases are baked into how the brain works.
What works is building SYSTEMS that counteract biases: checklists,
adversarial review, disconfirming evidence search, calibration tracking.

This catalog is the reference for those systems. Used by:
- Lens 06 (5-minute bias checklist)
- Adversarial audit (attack vectors)
- Pre-mortem (failure mode surfacing)
- Calibration protocol (tracking which biases you're most susceptible to)
</principle>

## Category 1: Information-Processing Biases

<information_biases>

### 1. Confirmation Bias
**What**: We seek evidence that confirms our beliefs and ignore disconfirming evidence.
**Business manifestation**: Founder reads positive customer feedback, ignores negative. Investor reads bullish thesis, ignores bearish signals.
**Counter-tactic**: Mandatory disconfirming evidence search (Lens 06). Ask "What would prove me wrong?" Adversarial audit.

### 2. Anchoring
**What**: The first number/idea anchors all subsequent thinking.
**Business manifestation**: First competitor's price becomes your pricing anchor. First TAM estimate anchors market sizing.
**Counter-tactic**: Re-derive from first principles. "If I'd never heard [anchoring number], what would I estimate?" Show expensive tier first (decoy effect, Lens 09).

### 3. Availability Heuristic
**What**: We judge probability by how easily examples come to mind.
**Business manifestation**: Recent vivid success feels more probable than it is. Recent failure feels more likely than it is.
**Counter-tactic**: Base rate thinking. "Of 100 opportunities like this, how many succeed?" Use data, not anecdotes.

### 4. Representativeness Heuristic
**What**: We judge probability by similarity to stereotype, not base rates.
**Business manifestation**: "This startup looks like Stripe, so it'll succeed like Stripe." Ignores base rate (95% of startups fail).
**Counter-tactic**: Always ask for base rate. Don't judge by similarity; judge by data.

### 5. Framing Effect
**What**: The same information framed differently leads to different decisions.
**Business manifestation**: "Save 20% with annual" beats "Pay 25% more monthly" (same math, different framing).
**Counter-tactic**: Reframe to test decisions. "Would I make this decision if framed as a loss instead of a gain?"

### 6. Hindsight Bias
**What**: After an event, we believe we predicted it ("I knew it all along").
**Business manifestation**: Founder reconstructs past predictions to match outcomes. Prevents learning from mistakes.
**Counter-tactic**: Decision journal (Lens 06). Write predictions BEFORE outcomes are known. Review honestly.

</information_biases>

## Category 2: Ego and Self-Perception Biases

<ego_biases>

### 7. Overconfidence Effect
**What**: We systematically overestimate our abilities, predictions, and control.
**Business manifestation**: 80% of founders believe they'll succeed; 95% fail. "I'm in the top 10% of drivers."
**Counter-tactic**: Calibration tracking. Record predictions, review accuracy. Apply 15-20% confidence discount by default.

### 8. Dunning-Kruger Effect
**What**: Incompetent people overestimate their competence. Competent people underestimate.
**Business manifestation**: Founder new to domain thinks they understand it. Founder expert in domain second-guesses themselves.
**Counter-tactic**: Recognize the curve. If you're new to a domain, assume you're overconfident. If you're expert, trust your judgment more.

### 9. Self-Serving Bias
**What**: We attribute success to ourselves, failure to external factors.
**Business manifestation**: "We succeeded because of great product." vs "We failed because of market timing."
**Counter-tactic**: Reverse attribution analysis. "If this failed, what would I blame externally? Is that actually external or did I contribute?"

### 10. Illusion of Control
**What**: We overestimate our ability to control outcomes.
**Business manifestation**: Founder believes hard work guarantees success. Ignores luck, market timing, external factors.
**Counter-tactic**: Acknowledge luck. Run pre-mortems. "What factors outside my control could cause failure?"

### 11. Sunk Cost Fallacy
**What**: We continue failing projects because of past investment.
**Business manifestation**: "We've invested 2 years, can't give up now." Keeps zombie projects alive.
**Counter-tactic**: Fang Yuan Axiom 1 (zero emotion). Ask: "Would I start this today if I hadn't already invested?" If no, kill.

### 12. Status Quo Bias
**What**: We prefer current state over change, even when change is better.
**Business manifestation**: "No decision" wins 60-70% of B2B deals. Customers stick with incumbent.
**Counter-tactic**: Quantify cost of inaction. Make switching cost low. Create urgency.

</ego_biases>

## Category 3: Social Biases

<social_biases>

### 13. Social Proof
**What**: We look to what others do when uncertain.
**Business manifestation**: "Everyone uses [tool], so it must be good." VCs fund what other VCs fund.
**Counter-tactic**: Distinguish validation (real customers paying) from herd behavior (everyone doing it because everyone's doing it).

### 14. Authority Bias
**What**: We defer to authority figures, even when they're wrong.
**Business manifestation**: "If [famous investor] invested, it must be good." "If [expert] says so, it must be true."
**Counter-tactic**: Evaluate the argument, not the arguer. Even experts have biases and blind spots.

### 15. Bandwagon Effect
**What**: We adopt beliefs because others hold them.
**Business manifestation**: "AI is hot, let's build an AI thing." Trend-chasing.
**Counter-tactic**: Lens 01 (Signal Scan) — distinguish structural signals from hype. Lens 10 (Competitor Teardown) — verify the wedge is real.

### 16. Groupthink
**What**: Groups prioritize harmony over critical thinking.
**Business manifestation**: Team agrees with founder because no one wants to disagree. Bad decisions go unchallenged.
**Counter-tactic**: Adversarial audit (Lens 06). Assign devil's advocate. "Disagree and commit" (Bezos).

### 17. Halo Effect
**What**: One positive trait colors perception of everything else.
**Business manifestation**: Well-designed website makes people assume product is good. Famous founder makes investors assume company will succeed.
**Counter-tactic**: Evaluate traits independently. Don't let one positive (design, brand, founder fame) override evaluation of fundamentals.

### 18. In-Group Bias
**What**: We favor people like us.
**Business manifestation**: "Culture fit" hiring = hiring people like me. Homogeneous teams.
**Counter-tactic**: Hire for culture ADD (what's missing), not culture FIT (what's comfortable). Structured interviews reduce bias.

</social_biases>

## Category 4: Probability and Prediction Biases

<probability_biases>

### 19. Planning Fallacy
**What**: We underestimate time and cost, overestimate benefits.
**Business manifestation**: Projects take 2x as long and cost 2x as much as planned. Revenue projections are optimistic.
**Counter-tactic**: Add 50% buffer to time and cost estimates. Reference class forecasting (look at similar projects' actuals).

### 20. Optimism Bias
**What**: We believe we're more likely to experience positive outcomes than base rates suggest.
**Business manifestation**: 80% of founders believe they'll succeed; 95% fail. Unrealistic revenue projections.
**Counter-tactic**: Base rate thinking. "What % of similar businesses succeed?" Apply that as your prior.

### 21. Loss Aversion
**What**: We fear loss 2x more than we value equivalent gain.
**Business manifestation**: Customers fight price increases more than they value decreases. People stay in bad jobs longer than they should.
**Counter-tactic**: Frame as loss in sales ("You're losing $X/month"). In decisions, recognize when loss aversion is keeping you in a bad situation.

### 22. Endowment Effect
**What**: We value things we own more than equivalent things we don't.
**Business manifestation**: Founder values their own idea more than equivalent opportunity. Won't sell underperforming asset.
**Counter-tactic**: "Would I buy this today at this price?" If no, sell. Don't let ownership distort value.

### 23. Survivorship Bias
**What**: We study successes and ignore failures.
**Business manifestation**: "How I built X" content. We see Stripe's success, not the 1000 failed payment startups.
**Counter-tactic**: Mandatory failure-case search (Lens 06). Search for "[idea] failed" not just "[idea] success."

### 24. Outcome Bias
**What**: We judge decisions by outcomes, not by quality of decision process.
**Business manifestation**: Lucky bad decision praised. Unlucky good decision criticized.
**Counter-tactic**: Evaluate decision process, not just outcome. Decision journal tracks process. Good process + bad outcome = still good process.

### 25. Hindsight Bias
**What**: After an event, we believe we predicted it.
**Business manifestation**: "I knew the market would crash." Reconstructs past predictions to match outcomes.
**Counter-tactic**: Decision journal. Write predictions BEFORE outcomes. Review accuracy honestly.

</probability_biases>

## Category 5: Memory and Recall Biases

<memory_biases>

### 26. Recency Bias
**What**: We overweight recent events.
**Business manifestation**: Last quarter's strong performance feels more predictive than 2-year trend. Recent failure feels more likely to repeat.
**Counter-tactic**: Look at multi-year trends, not just recent data. "What's the pattern over 24 months, not 3?"

### 27. Peak-End Rule
**What**: We judge experiences by their peak and end, not average.
**Business manifestation**: Customer remembers onboarding friction and last support interaction, not the 100 good days in between.
**Counter-tactic**: Optimize peak moments and end-of-experience. Don't let the last interaction be bad.

### 28. Misinformation Effect
**What**: Post-event information distorts memory of the event.
**Business manifestation**: After hearing a competitor's pitch, you misremember your customer's actual complaint.
**Counter-tactic**: Document in real-time. Don't rely on memory for important data.

</memory_biases>

## Category 6: Business-Specific Biases

<business_biases>

### 29. Survivorship Bias in Opportunity Analysis
**What**: We see successful companies and assume their model works, ignoring the failures.
**Business manifestation**: "Stripe succeeded with developer-focused payments, so I will too." Ignores 1000 failed payment startups.
**Counter-tactic**: Search for failures explicitly. "What payment startups failed? Why?" (Lens 06 disconfirming evidence search)

### 30. Narrative Fallacy
**What**: We construct causal stories from random events.
**Business manifestation**: "Stripe succeeded because of [specific decision]." Ignores luck, timing, and the 100 decisions that didn't matter.
**Counter-tactic**: Recognize retrospective narratives are suspect. Focus on base rates and structural advantages, not stories.

### 31. Not Invented Here Syndrome
**What**: We undervalue ideas/solutions from outside our organization.
**Business manifestation**: "We need to build it ourselves." Even when off-the-shelf solutions exist.
**Counter-tactic**: Default to "buy" over "build." Only build when off-the-shelf doesn't meet needs.

### 32. Feature Factory Bias
**What**: We equate shipping features with progress.
**Business manifestation**: "We shipped 50 features this quarter!" Ignores whether features moved metrics.
**Counter-tactic**: Measure outcomes, not output. "What metrics moved?" not "What did we ship?"

### 33. Vanity Metrics Bias
**What**: We track metrics that look good but don't reflect business health.
**Business manifestation**: "We have 100K registered users!" (but 5K active). "We have 1M pageviews!" (but 1K conversions).
**Counter-tactic**: Track actionable metrics (AARRR). Active users, not registered. Conversion rate, not traffic. MRR, not pipeline.

### 34. Sunk Cost in Hiring
**What**: We keep bad hires because we invested in recruiting/onboarding them.
**Business manifestation**: "We spent 3 months hiring them, can't fire now." Keeps bad hires, poisons team.
**Counter-tactic**: Performance plan (30 days), then fire. Don't let sunk cost in recruiting extend sunk cost in employment.

### 35. Founder Bias
**What**: Founders believe their judgment is better than the data.
**Business manifestation**: "I know our customers want X." Despite data showing they want Y.
**Counter-tactic**: Data-driven decisions. When founder intuition conflicts with data, run an experiment. Don't override data with intuition.

</business_biases>

## The Anti-Bias System

<anti_bias_system>

### The 5-Check Bias Audit (Lens 06)
Run before every important decision:

1. **Confirmation bias check**: What evidence would prove me wrong?
2. **Sunk cost check**: Would I start this today if I hadn't already?
3. **Anchoring check**: Am I anchored to the first number/idea?
4. **Availability check**: Is this based on recent events or base rates?
5. **Overconfidence check**: What's my 90% confidence interval?

If 2+ checks fail → downgrade PASS to FLAG, FLAG to REJECT.

### The Adversarial Audit (Lens 06)
After bias checklist, run adversarial pass:
- Adversary's job is to kill the idea
- Constructs 3-5 specific attacks
- Runs disconfirming evidence searches
- Returns verdict: KILLED / SURVIVED / COULDN'T BREAK IT

### The Decision Journal (Lens 06)
Record predictions BEFORE outcomes:
- Prediction, confidence, assumptions, kill criteria
- Review accuracy after outcome
- Compute calibration over time
- Adjust confidence for known biases

### The Calibration Loop (calibration-protocol.md)
Monthly/quarterly/annual reviews:
- Compute per-lens accuracy
- Identify recurring bias patterns
- Apply confidence adjustments to future analyses

## Why Most Bias Training Fails

<why_training_fails>
Most bias training tries to teach people to recognize biases in real-time.
This fails because:
1. **Biases operate in System 1 (fast, automatic)** — by the time you recognize
   a bias, you've already acted on it
2. **Recognition doesn't prevent action** — knowing you're overconfident doesn't
   make you less overconfident
3. **Training decays** — without constant practice, bias recognition fades

What works:
1. **Systems, not awareness** — checklists, adversarial review, calibration
2. **Structural changes** — decision journals, mandatory disconfirming search
3. **Accountability** — review past decisions honestly, track calibration

The skill uses systems, not awareness. That's why it works.
</why_training_fails>
