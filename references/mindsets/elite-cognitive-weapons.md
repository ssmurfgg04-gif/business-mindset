# Elite Cognitive Weapons — The Top 1% Decision Engine

This framework codifies the mental weapons that separate the top 1% from the top
10%. Talent and effort are table stakes. What differentiates the elite is how they
think under uncertainty, when money is on the line, and when ego wants to win.

Load this framework for any high-stakes decision, contrarian positioning,
fundraising, negotiation, or PASS-level commitment. It is the cognitive layer
between raw analysis and action.

---

## 1. Probabilistic Thinking & Expected Value (EV Over Ego)

The amateur asks "is this a good idea?" (binary). The elite asks "what are the
odds and payoffs?" (distribution).

- **Never decide in yes/no — decide in odds.** Convert every binary instinct into
  a probability estimate. "I'm 70% confident this works, with a 10x payoff and a
  90% chance of partial failure on the first iteration."
- **EV = P(win) × Size(win) − P(lose) × Size(lose).** Compute it crudely and
  consciously, even with rough numbers. The discipline matters more than precision.
- **Volume beats accuracy on reversible bets.** When downside is capped and
  reversible (Optionality pillar), take more shots with lower conviction. When a
  bet is irreversible and ruin-possible, require high confidence before acting.
- **Anti-pattern:** "I believe in this" as a substitute for computing EV. Belief
  without a number is ego wearing a strategy costume.

## 2. Bayesian Updating — Revise on Evidence, Not Attachment

The amateur defends a position. The elite updates it.

- **Prior → Evidence → Posterior.** State your belief before seeing new data,
  then let the data move it. A belief that cannot be moved by evidence is not a
  belief — it is an identity.
- **Ask "what would change my mind?" BEFORE committing.** If you cannot answer,
  you are not analyzing — you are rationalizing.
- **Weigh disconfirming evidence MORE than confirming evidence.** The skill's
  mandatory disconfirming-evidence search is the institutionalized form of this.
  A single strong piece of counter-evidence beats three weak pieces of support.
- **Update speed is a competitive weapon.** The market punishes slow learners.
  The elite revise within days of new information; the amateur waits for
  vindication.

---

## 3. Second-Order Thinking (Munger: "And Then What?")

The amateur stops at first-order consequences. The elite traces the chain.

- **First-order:** "Raise prices → higher revenue."
- **Second-order:** "Higher price → fewer customers → but the ones who stay are
  more serious → support cost drops → we can build premium features → word-of-mouth
  improves with the right segment."
- **Third-order:** "Premium repositioning → new competitors appear from above →
  need distribution relationships we don't have → therefore build the partner
  channel BEFORE repricing."
- **Practice rule:** Every time you reach a conclusion, ask "and then what?"
  at least twice. If the second-order result is unacceptable, the first-order
  action is wrong regardless of how good it looks.

---

## 4. Inversion — Plan the Failure First

The Stoics, Munger, and every great operator share one habit: they plan the
catastrophe before the victory.

- **Start the pre-mortem before the case FOR.** Write "we failed in 6 months"
  and list the 3-5 most likely causes, with the leading indicator for each.
  Only then argue for the opportunity.
- **Kill criteria are written BEFORE money is spent.** A commitment without a
  pre-committed kill threshold is a hope, not a strategy.
- **Invert the question.** "How do we succeed?" → "How do we guarantee failure?"
  The failure list is usually more honest and more actionable.
- **Use inversion for pricing too.** "How would we guarantee underpricing?"
  → set prices based on cost instead of value. Then do the opposite.

## 5. Negotiation Psychology Foundation

The weapon behind every negotiation (deepened in `frameworks/sales-and-negotiation-mastery.md`):

- **BATNA is everything.** Your leverage = your Best Alternative To Negotiated
  Agreement. Never enter a negotiation without a walk-away option. The party
  who can leave comfortably controls the outcome.
- **Anchor before being anchored.** Whoever names the number first sets the frame.
  The elite anchor high but justifiable; the amateur negotiates against their
  own first number.
- **Patience is leverage.** Time pressure is the negotiator's enemy. The party
  who can wait wins. Refuse to signal urgency.
- **Never split the difference on principle.** Splitting rewards the worse
  position. Find the objective criteria ("market rate", "comparable deals") and
  negotiate against the standard, not against the person.

---

## 6. The Ego Kill Switch

Ego is the single most expensive cognitive error in business. It converts
evidence into self-defense and losses into identity.

- **Verdicts follow evidence, not identity.** If the analysis says your idea is
  bad, the idea is bad — not you. Detach self-worth from being right.
- **The pride tax:** every time you hold a position to avoid admitting error,
  you pay compounding interest on the original mistake. The elite eat the loss
  fast, in public, and move on.
- **Zero emotion, zero ego (Fang Yuan floor).** The evaluation of an opportunity
  must be indistinguishable from evaluating a stranger's opportunity. Same
  standard, same ruthlessness, same honesty.
- **Confidence vs. correctness:** confidence is about capability ("I can figure
  this out"), not certainty ("I am right"). High capability-confidence with low
  position-certainty is the elite posture.

---

## 7. Calm Under Ambiguity

Knightian uncertainty is not a flaw to be eliminated — it is a field to be
exploited.

- **Comfort with not-knowing is a competitive weapon.** Most operators freeze
  when the answer is unknowable. The elite make the best decision with the best
  available information and commit, while keeping optionality.
- **Decision speed under ambiguity beats decision quality delayed.**
  A good decision now beats a perfect decision later when conditions change
  fast. Reversible bets should be taken fast; irreversible bets deserve the
  deliberate lane.
- **The ambiguity posture:** "I don't know, therefore I will (a) learn cheaply,
  (b) keep the option open, (c) not commit more than I can lose, and (d) not
  pretend certainty." This is the anti-fragile default.
- **Protect the emotional baseline.** Panic, euphoria, and desperation all
  degrade the same decision engine. When you feel either extreme, defer the
  decision or shrink the bet until the baseline returns.

---

## Integration Rules

- **Before every PASS verdict:** run the 3-second ego check — "Am I saying this
  because it's true, or because I want it to be true?"
- **Before every execution commitment:** run second-order thinking to depth 2,
  and write the kill criteria first (inversion).
- **During every negotiation:** name your BATNA, anchor deliberately, and refuse
  to signal urgency.
- **After every outcome:** update the Bayesian prior in the calibration file.
  A skill that never revises its own beliefs is not elite — it is rigid.
