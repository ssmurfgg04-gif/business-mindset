## Growth by Stage: Quick Reference

<stage_quick_reference>

### Stage 0: Pre-PMF ($0 → $10K MRR)
**Goal**: Find product-market fit. Do NOT scale.

**Tactics**:
- Founder-led customer development (10-20 customer interviews/month)
- Manual sales (no SDRs, no marketing)
- Manual onboarding (no self-serve yet)
- Charge from day 1 (free users don't give honest feedback)
- Iterate weekly based on customer feedback

**Anti-tactics (don't do)**:
- Paid acquisition (CAC catastrophic without PMF)
- Hiring sales (you don't know the pitch yet)
- Hiring marketing (you don't know what to say)
- Building growth features (no user base)
- Scaling operations (stay manual)

**Exit criteria**: Sean Ellis test ≥40%, retention flattens, <5% monthly churn, $10K+ MRR

### Stage 1: Early PMF ($10K → $1M MRR)
**Goal**: Double down on ONE channel. Don't diversify.

**Tactics**:
- Identify dominant channel (from Lens 05)
- Hire ONE specialist for that channel
- Build the playbook for that channel, document everything
- Founder still does enterprise sales
- Start cohort tracking (by channel, by segment)
- Raise prices annually (5-10%)

**Anti-tactics**:
- Diversifying channels (one channel at 3:1 beats five at 1:1)
- Hiring VP of Sales (still figuring out the motion)
- Building a "growth team" (Stage 2 luxury)
- Raising big rounds ($1-3M max, stay capital-efficient)
- Expanding to new segments (nail current ICP first)

**Exit criteria**: $1M+ ARR, one channel with 3:1+ LTV:CAC, CAC payback <12 months, NRR ≥100%

### Stage 2: Scaling ($1M → $10M MRR)
**Goal**: Replicate the proven motion. Add second channel only when first is saturated.

**Tactics**:
- Hire VP of Sales (SLG) or VP of Growth (PLG)
- Build the sales playbook, replicate founder's process
- Add second channel (after first saturated)
- Build customer success team (NRR becomes critical)
- Self-serve onboarding (PLG) or sales-assisted funnel (SLG)
- International expansion (if applicable)

**Anti-tactics**:
- Third channel (two is plenty, three = dilution)
- Multiple products (stay focused on core)
- "Chief Strategy Officer" (execute, don't strategize)
- Crazy valuations (capital efficiency compounds)

**Exit criteria**: $10M+ ARR, two channels with proven unit economics, Magic Number ≥0.5, Rule of 40 ≥40

### Stage 3: Scale ($10M → $100M MRR)
**Goal**: Multi-channel scaling + product expansion.

**Tactics**:
- Add 3rd and 4th channels (events, ABM, partnerships, international)
- Launch adjacent products
- Build executive team (CRO, CMO, CPO, CFO)
- Geographic expansion (US → EU → APAC)
- Consider M&A

**Anti-tactics**:
- Going public prematurely (IPO readiness = 2-3 years prep)
- Losing Day 1 mentality (Bezos: "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by death.")

</stage_quick_reference>

## Distribution Channel Deep-Dive

<channels>

### SEO
**When it works**: High-intent queries, considered purchase, B2B
**CAC range**: $50-500
**Time to results**: 6-18 months (slow, then compounds)
**Key tactics**:
- Topic clusters (pillar + supporting content)
- Backlink building (HARO, guest posts, linkable assets)
- Technical SEO (site speed, structured data, mobile-first)
- Content velocity (4-8 articles/month minimum)
- Rank tracking (Ahrefs, Semrush)

**Common mistakes**:
- Targeting head terms (too competitive)
- Ignoring technical SEO (slow site kills rankings)
- Inconsistent publishing (algorithm rewards consistency)
- No CTA on content (traffic doesn't convert)

### Content Marketing
**When it works**: B2B considered purchase, education-led sales, brand building
**CAC range**: $100-1000
**Time to results**: 6-12 months
**Key tactics**:
- Pillar content (comprehensive guides, 2000+ words)
- Distribution > creation (50% of effort on distribution)
- Repurpose across channels (blog → Twitter → LinkedIn → email → video)
- Build email list (owned distribution)
- Measure leads, not just traffic

**Common mistakes**:
- Publishing without distribution (build it and they won't come)
- No CTA (readers don't know what to do next)
- No measurement (can't improve what you don't track)
- Inconsistent (one post a quarter = no momentum)

### Paid Acquisition — Google Ads
**When it works**: High-intent search, clear value prop, strong landing page
**CAC range**: $100-2000
**Time to results**: 1-2 weeks (immediate, then optimize)
**Key tactics**:
- Single keyword ad groups (SKAGs)
- Negative keywords (filter junk)
- Landing page testing (3+ variants)
- Conversion tracking (essential, not optional)
- Bid strategy: Target CPA or Maximize Conversions

**Common mistakes**:
- Broad match (wastes money on irrelevant queries)
- No conversion tracking (flying blind)
- No landing page testing (one page = no learning)
- One ad per group (no creative testing)

### Paid Acquisition — Meta/TikTok
**When it works**: B2C, impulse purchase, visual products
**CAC range**: $20-300
**Time to results**: 2-4 weeks
**Key tactics**:
- Creative testing (3+ new creatives per week)
- Lookalike audiences (based on customer list)
- Retargeting (95% of converters see retargeting first)
- Video > image (especially TikTok)
- UGC-style creative (outperforms polished)

**Common mistakes**:
- One creative (creative fatigue kills performance)
- No retargeting (leaving 70% of conversions on table)
- Broad audience (use lookalikes)
- Polished corporate creative (looks like an ad, gets ignored)

### Outbound Sales
**When it works**: B2B, high ACV (>$10K/yr), defined ICP
**CAC range**: $500-5000
**Time to results**: 1-3 months (long sales cycles)
**Reply rate benchmarks**: Cold email 3-5% (good), 5-10% (excellent). LinkedIn 10-20%.
**Key tactics**:
- Personalized outreach (not templates — reference their company, their work)
- Multi-touch (email + LinkedIn + call, 8-12 touches over 2-3 weeks)
- Follow-up (80% of conversions happen after 5th touch)
- ICP definition (specific: industry, size, role, pain)
- Sales navigator + Apollo + Lemlist stack

**Common mistakes**:
- Generic templates (immediately deleted)
- No follow-up (80% of value lost)
- No ICP definition (spray and pray)
- Pitching in first email (build rapport first)

### Partnerships
**When it works**: Complementary products, shared ICP
**CAC range**: $50-500 (lower because shared)
**Time to results**: 3-6 months (relationship building)
**Key tactics**:
- Co-marketing (webinars, ebooks, events)
- Integration partnerships (technical integration + co-selling)
- Reseller agreements (they sell your product)
- Affiliate programs (10-30% commission)
- Find partners with same ICP, non-competing product

**Common mistakes**:
- One-sided partnerships (what's in it for them?)
- No clear value to partner
- No measurement (partner "brings customers" but can't prove it)
- No dedicated partner manager

### Influencer Marketing
**When it works**: B2C, visual products, niche audiences
**CAC range**: $50-1000
**Time to results**: 1-2 months
**Key tactics**:
- Micro-influencers (1K-100K followers) — higher engagement, lower cost
- Affiliate model (pay per conversion, not per post)
- Authentic content (let them create, don't script)
- Long-term partnerships (one-offs don't build trust)
- Track with promo codes + UTM links

**Common mistakes**:
- Mega-influencers (low engagement, high cost)
- Pay-per-post (no incentive for conversion)
- No tracking (can't measure ROI)
- Scripted content (looks like an ad, gets ignored)

### Community-Led Growth
**When it works**: Developer tools, technical products, niche professional communities
**CAC range**: $20-200 (low because community attracts)
**Time to results**: 6-18 months (slow build)
**Key tactics**:
- Discord/Slack community (where your audience already is)
- Open-source (if applicable — GitHub stars as distribution)
- Events (meetups, conferences, virtual events)
- Content (community-generated content scales)
- Community manager (dedicated role, not side project)

**Common mistakes**:
- Treating community as marketing channel (it's a product)
- No community manager (communities die without active management)
- No value to free members (they leave)
- Spamming community with promotions (kills trust)

### Product-Led Growth (PLG)
**When it works**: Self-serve product, clear aha moment, viral or sticky mechanics
**CAC range**: $20-200 (low because product acquires)
**Time to results**: 3-6 months to see flywheel
**Key tactics**:
- Free tier or trial (low friction to start)
- In-product onboarding (no human needed)
- Usage-based pricing (pay as you grow)
- Viral loops (invite teammates, share publicly)
- Time-to-aha <5 minutes (Slack: 2,000 messages = aha)

**Common mistakes**:
- Free tier too generous (no reason to upgrade, expensive to host)
- No onboarding (drop-off at signup)
- No expansion (no upsell mechanism)
- Free tier that solves the whole problem (no upgrade trigger)

### Sales-Led Growth (SLG)
**When it works**: High ACV (>$50K/yr), complex product, enterprise buyers
**CAC range**: $5K-50K (high but justified by ACV)
**Time to results**: 3-9 months (long sales cycles)
**Key tactics**:
- SDR → AE → CSM structure (specialization)
- Account-based marketing (ABM) — target specific accounts
- Demo-led sales (custom demos for each prospect)
- Sales ops (CRM, sequences, enablement)
- Free pilot/POC (let them try before buying)

**Common mistakes**:
- Hiring SDRs too early (before AE playbook is proven)
- No ICP definition (ABM fails without target list)
- No sales ops (AEs waste time on admin)
- Pitching features instead of outcomes

## Metrics Reference

<metrics>
| Metric | Formula | Benchmark | When it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sean Ellis test | % "very disappointed" without product | ≥40% = PMF | Pre-PMF |
| NRR | (Starting MRR + expansion - churn - downgrade) / Starting MRR | ≥110% enterprise, ≥100% SMB | Stage 1+ |
| GRR | (Starting MRR - churn - downgrade) / Starting MRR | ≥90% | Stage 1+ |
| LTV:CAC | LTV / CAC | ≥3:1 | Stage 1+ |
| CAC payback | CAC / (monthly gross margin per customer) | <12 months | Stage 1+ |
| Magic Number | (Quarterly ARR growth × 4) / (prior quarter S&M spend) | ≥0.5 good, ≥1.0 excellent | Stage 2+ |
| Rule of 40 | Growth rate % + profit margin % | ≥40 | Stage 2+ |
| Burn multiple | Net burn / net new ARR | <1.5 good, <1.0 excellent | Stage 1+ |
| Gross margin | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | ≥70% SaaS | All stages |
| Monthly churn | Customers lost / starting customers | <5% SMB, <2% enterprise | All stages |
| NPS | % promoters - % detractors | ≥40 good, ≥50 excellent | All stages |
| Cohort retention | % of cohort still active at month N | Flattens = PMF | Pre-PMF, ongoing |

### SaaS Scale Statistics (reality check)
- 4% of SaaS surpass $1M ARR
- 0.04% reach $10M ARR
- 0.0005% reach $100M ARR
- Most SaaS plateau at $1-3M ARR

Source: KeyBanc SaaS survey.
</metrics>

## Customer Success Playbook

<customer_success>
### Aha Moments (examples)
- Slack: 2,000 messages sent → 93% retention
- Facebook: 7 friends in 10 days
- Dropbox: 1 file in 1 folder on 1 device
- Twitter: follow 30 people
- Zoom: 1st meeting with 3+ participants

### Onboarding Framework
1. **Time-to-value**: <5 minutes from signup to first "wow"
2. **Progressive disclosure**: don't show all features, show the next step
3. **Empty states**: guide users when they have no data
4. **In-app nudges**: tooltips, checklists, walkthroughs
5. **Human fallback**: offer chat/call for users who get stuck

### Retention Curves
- **PMF curve**: flattens at >60% retention (good)
- **No PMF curve**: continues declining to 0% (bad)
- **Negative churn**: curve goes UP over time (expansion > churn) — the goal

### Churn Analysis
- **Gross churn**: customers who left
- **Net churn**: customers who left minus expansion from retained customers
- **Segment churn**: by customer segment (SMB vs enterprise, by industry)
- **Cohort churn**: by signup cohort (when did they sign up?)
- **Churn reasons**: exit interviews, NPS detractors, support tickets

### Expansion Revenue
- Upsell (more seats, higher tier)
- Cross-sell (additional products)
- Usage-based expansion (more usage = more revenue)
- Price increases (annual, see Lens 09)

### Customer Health Score
- Usage frequency (login count, feature usage)
- Support tickets (high = at risk)
- NPS score (detractors at risk)
- Payment method (failed payments = at risk)
- Account changes (champion left, company downsized)

**Action**: Health score < threshold → CSM outreach. Don't wait for churn.
</customer_success>

## Common Failure Modes (Top 20 from CB Insights)

<failure_modes>
1. **No market need** (42%) — building something nobody wants. Fix: customer development, Lens 02.
2. **Ran out of cash** (29%) — poor financial management. Fix: Lens 08, tax reserves.
3. **Wrong team** (23%) — co-founder conflict, bad hires. Fix: Lens 12, reference checks.
4. **Got outcompeted** (19%) — better competitor won. Fix: Lens 10, counter-positioning.
5. **Pricing/cost issues** (18%) — underpricing, high costs. Fix: Lens 09.
6. **Poor product** (17%) — buggy, missing features. Fix: ship quality, listen to users.
7. **Need business model** (17%) — no clear revenue path. Fix: Lens 09, business model canvas.
8. **Poor marketing** (14%) — distribution failure. Fix: Lens 05, Lens 13.
9. **Ignore customers** (14%) — built without talking to users. Fix: customer development.
10. **Product mistimed** (13%) — too early or too late. Fix: Lens 01, timing analysis.
11. **Lose focus** (13%) — too many things at once. Fix: Lens 11, Lens 13.
12. **Disharmony team** (13%) — co-founder disputes. Fix: co-founder agreement.
13. **Pivot gone bad** (10%) — wrong pivot direction. Fix: data-driven pivots.
14. **Burn out** (8%) — founder exhaustion. Fix: Lens 08 (burnout check).
15. **Failure to pivot** (7%) — too stubborn. Fix: pre-defined reversal criteria.
16. **Poor investor relations** (4%) — board conflict. Fix: transparent communication.
17. **Legal challenges** (3%) — regulatory or IP issues. Fix: compliance counsel.
18. **Don't use network** (2%) — didn't leverage connections. Fix: Lens 05.
19. **Burn through cash** (2%) — excessive burn rate. Fix: Lens 08.
20. **Don't adapt** (1%) — stuck in original plan. Fix: "new CEO" mindset.

### Additional failure patterns (beyond CB Insights)
- **Premature scaling** (74% per Startup Genome Project) — scaling before PMF
- **Co-founder conflict** (65% per Wasserman) — usually equity or role disputes
- **Single founder failure** — too lonely, no one to challenge decisions
</failure_modes>
