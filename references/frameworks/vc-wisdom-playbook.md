# VC Wisdom & Operator Playbook

Synthesizing core mental models, frameworks, and operating principles from top venture capital firms (Y Combinator, Sequoia Capital, a16z) and legendary tech founders/investors (Paul Graham, Marc Andreessen, Peter Thiel, Ben Horowitz).

---

## 1. Y Combinator / Paul Graham Playbook

- **Do Things That Don't Scale**: In the early stages, manually onboard users, provide white-glove service, and do unscalable things to learn what customers actually want. Momentum beats elegance.
- **Default Alive vs. Default Dead**: Calculate your burn rate and revenue growth. If you run out of money before hitting profitability at current growth rates, you are "default dead" and must cut costs or raise capital immediately. "Default alive" gives you total leverage.
- **Make Something People Want**: The single most common cause of startup failure is building something nobody wants. Obsess over user demand before writing code.
- **Live in the Future, Then Build What's Missing**: Look at what tech elites or outliers are doing today that seems weird, and build the infrastructure for it.
- **The Hair on Fire Problem**: Build solutions for problems so acute that customers feel like their hair is on fire. They won't care about UI bugs; they just want relief.

---

## 2. Sequoia Capital Playbook

- **The Burning Pain & Market Size**: Is the pain severe, frequent, and growing? Is the market large enough (TAM) to support venture returns via power law dynamics?
- **Founders with Obsession & Resilience**: Look for founders who possess unshakeable conviction, deep domain insight, and extreme psychological resilience in the face of near-death experiences.
- **The Unfair Advantage**: What do you know, own, or execute that competitors cannot easily copy within 24 months? (Proprietary data, unique distribution, elite engineering talent).
- **Simplicity and Focus**: The best pitches and business models can be explained in one compelling sentence. Complexity is a symptom of muddled thinking.

---

## 3. Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) Playbook

- **Wartime vs. Peacetime CEOs**: 
  - *Peacetime CEO*: Focuses on expanding markets, fine-tuning culture, and delegation.
  - *Wartime CEO*: Focuses on survival, brutal execution, decisive elimination of weak links, and cash preservation. Most startups are perpetually at war.
- **Hire for Strength, Not Absence of Weakness**: In business, you don't get points for balance. Hire world-class specialists who excel brilliantly in one critical domain, even if they have flaws elsewhere.
- **Product-Market Fit (PMF)**: "The customers are buying the product just as fast as you can make it... money from customers is piling up in your company checking account." Until you have PMF, nothing else matters.
- **Take Smart Risks**: In high-uncertainty environments, conservatism is riskier than calculated aggression. Speed of experimentation beats perfection.

---

## 4. Application to the Business-Mindset Engine

When evaluating any venture through this lens:
1. **The PG Test**: Is the hair on fire? Are you doing things that don't scale to get the first 10 customers?
2. **The Sequoia Test**: Is this a power-law market or a linear lifestyle business?
3. **The a16z Test**: Have you achieved undeniable product-market fit before pouring fuel on growth?
