# ECR Model — Expansion, Contraction, Realism

Source: Ramoglou, S., Chandra, Y., & Jin, Q. (2026). *Opportunity Search in

DOI: 10.1111/joms.70011.

## Core Insight

In the GenAI era, the bottleneck of entrepreneurship is **not** scarcity of
ideas — it's **Knightian Uncertainty** (the inability to know which AI-generated
futures are possible vs. imaginary). More ideas = more noise, not more signal.

The solution: a **symbiotic division of epistemic labor** between machine and
human intelligence. AI generates possibility spaces; humans ground them in
ontological reality.

## The Three Phases

### Phase 1: Expansion (Machine Leads)

**Goal**: Generate a large, diverse ideation space via generative variation.

**Quantity breeds quality heuristic** (Simonton, 1999): The probability that
real opportunities are discovered increases with the size of the ideation space.

**AI's role**: Use web research, data analysis, pattern recognition to generate
a broad field of potential opportunity spaces. At this stage, DO NOT filter.

**MANDATORY QUOTA**: Generate **15-20+** raw candidates per lens before any
contraction begins. Do NOT collapse to "top 3-5" prematurely. The single most
common failure mode is premature contraction — the AI defaults to the 3 most
familiar patterns and discards the unconventional ones that might be the
actual opportunity.

**Knightian Uncertainty warning**: The expansion phase produces opportunities
that are **plausible but not yet actualizable**. Many will be figments of
machine imagination — plausible-sounding futures that don't correspond to
anything realizable. This is fine. The contraction phase filters them.

**Output**: A field of 15-20+ potential opportunities, ranked by plausibility
(not profitability). Include the weakest ones — they often reveal adjacent
spaces. Each candidate gets a one-line description and a source citation.

### Phase 2: Contraction (Human Leads, AI Supports)

**Goal**: Eliminate non-opportunities systematically.

**Weak link principle**: It's easier to identify why an idea WON'T work than
to confirm it WILL. A single missing "opportunity ingredient" suffices to
eliminate it. This makes elimination a cheaper, more reliable epistemic strategy
than attempted validation.

**Human's role**: Apply judgment, domain expertise, and contextual knowledge
to eliminate what can't be actualized. The human provides **ontological
intelligence** — common-sense understanding of what's real that the AI lacks.

**AI's role**: For each opportunity, identify potential weak links:
- No clear buyer
- Unsustainable unit economics
- Requires permission you don't have
- Competition too entrenched
- Timing wrong (too early or too late)
- Regulatory barrier
- Requires capital you don't have access to

**Output**: A shortlist of 3-5 opportunities that survive elimination, plus
explicit kill reasons for the 10-15 that did not. The kill reasons are
valuable — they document what was considered and rejected, so future
sessions don't re-litigate.

### Phase 3: Realism (Ecological Rationality)

**Goal**: Match remaining opportunities to the actual decision environment.

**Ecological rationality principle** (Gigerenzer's paradigm): Decision strategies
should be attuned to the structure of the environment, not idealized models of
perfect rationality. There is no single "correct" decision strategy — what works
depends on the environment's statistical structure.

**Checks**:
- Does this opportunity match the user's actual resources (time, capital, skills)?
  If no intake provided, default to "average solo operator, $0 capital, 10-15 hrs/week."
- Does the user have genuine interest/domain knowledge here?
- Can this be tested with zero capital in under 7 days?
- What's the specific path to first paying customer?
- What's the actual competition (not theoretical)?
- Which decision heuristic fits this environment?

**Output**: 1-3 actionable opportunity briefs with concrete next steps, scored
against the 6 Pillars (Asymmetric Execution) and the 10 Signals (Lens 07
Exponential Potential). Each includes a pre-mortem.

## Application to This Skill

The ECR model maps directly to the lens pipeline:

```
Phase 1 (EXPANSION) — generate 15-20+ per lens:
  Lens 01: Signal Scan ─── Generates many raw market signals
  Lens 02: Demand Gap  ─── Generates unmet need patterns
  Lens 07: Exponential Potential ─── (used in evaluation, not generation)

Phase 2 (CONTRACTION) — apply weak-link elimination, reduce to 3-5:
  Lens 03: Arbitrage Pattern ─── Eliminates non-arbitrageable signals
  Lens 06: Anti-Bias Audit  ─── Eliminates familiar/saturated plays

Phase 3 (REALISM) — match to actual environment:
  Lens 04: Leverage Map ─── Matches to user's actual leverage options
  Lens 05: Network Path ─── Matches to user's actual distribution options
  Lens 07: Exponential Potential ─── Ranks survivors by tier
```

## Enforcement Mechanism

To prevent premature contraction (the #1 failure mode), the skill enforces:

1. **Quota check**: If expansion output has <15 candidates per lens, the agent
   MUST generate more before proceeding to contraction. Do not skip this.
2. **Kill-log requirement**: Every candidate eliminated in contraction must
   have a one-sentence kill reason recorded. If you can't articulate why you
   killed it, you can't kill it.
3. **Revival clause**: If all 3-5 contraction survivors later fail realism
   checks, the agent returns to the expansion pool and re-evaluates 3-5
   previously-killed candidates with explicit justification for revival.
4. **Anti-pattern alert**: If the final 3-5 survivors are all "familiar
   defaults" (newsletter, SaaS, marketplace, freelance), the agent MUST
   re-expand with explicit instruction to find non-default candidates.

## Practical Use

1. Start with **expansion mindset**: Don't filter early. Let the research
   surface many possibilities, including improbable ones. Generate 15-20+
   per lens, no exceptions.
2. Switch to **contraction mindset**: Systematically eliminate. For each
   possibility, ask "what single missing ingredient kills this?" If you find it,
   discard. If you can't find one, keep it. Record the kill reason.
3. End with **realism**: Before presenting any opportunity, ask: "Does this
   actually fit the user's situation, or is it just theoretically interesting?"
4. **Force re-expansion if needed**: If the survivor pool looks too familiar,
   too narrow, or too saturated, return to Phase 1 with a directive to find
   non-default candidates.

## Key Warnings

- **Avoid premature contraction**: The most common mistake is to start
  eliminating before you've expanded enough. The 15-20+ quota exists to
  prevent this. Enforce it.
- **Avoid expansion without direction** (spray and pray): Use the lenses to
  structure the expansion phase, don't just randomly search.
- **Human judgement is irreplaceable in contraction**: The AI can identify
  weak links, but only a human can weigh them in context. The final cut is
  human.
- **Knightian Uncertainty is not a bug**: The fact that AI generates many
  unrealizable futures is inherent to language models. The skill's value is in
  systematically distinguishing which futures are real.
- **Survivor bias in the kill log**: Be willing to revisit killed candidates
  if the survivors all fail realism. Don't let the kill log become a graveyard.
