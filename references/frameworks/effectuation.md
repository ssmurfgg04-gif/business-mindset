# Effectuation — Means-Driven Entrepreneurial Logic

Source: Sarasvathy, S.D. (2001). *Causation and effectuation: Toward a
theoretical shift from economic inevitability to entrepreneurial contingency.*
Academy of Management Review, 26(2), 243-263.

**10,549+ citations** (the most-cited entrepreneurship paper of the last 25
years). Sarasvathy won the 2022 Global Award for Entrepreneurship Research.

## Core Insight

Expert entrepreneurs do **not** start with a goal and find resources to achieve
it (causation). They start with their available means and imagine possible
effects (effectuation). The question is not "what should I create?" but "what
can I create with what I have?"

## The 5 Effectuation Principles

### 1. Bird-in-Hand (Means-Driven)

Start with three questions:
- **Who I am** (traits, tastes, abilities)
- **What I know** (education, expertise, experience)
- **Whom I know** (social networks, relationships)

**Application**: Most opportunity frameworks assume you can acquire any
resource. Effectuation says: work with what you already have. This maps to
the **Leverage Map** lens (04) — start at the zero-capital end of the
spectrum before considering capital-intensive plays.

### 2. Affordable Loss (Downside Constraint)

Invest only what you're willing to lose. Never project returns — calculate
maximum downside instead.

**Application**: This is a direct input to the **Anti-Bias Audit** (Lens 06)
Capital Check. Instead of asking "how much could I make?", ask "what's the
most I can lose, and can I absorb it?"

### 3. Crazy Quilt (Partnerships)

Commit to partnerships with self-selecting stakeholders before knowing the
full opportunity. Let the network define the opportunity.

**Application**: Maps to **Network Path** lens (05). Don't wait until you
have a perfect product to find partners. Find partners first; they'll help
shape what's viable.

### 4. Lemonade Principle (Contingency)

Embrace surprises rather than avoiding them. Bad news is an opportunity to
pivot.

**Application**: When the **Signal Scan** (Lens 01) finds data that
contradicts your initial hypothesis, don't discard it — investigate. The
signal is the opportunity.

### 5. Pilot-in-the-Plane (Human Agency)

Focus on what you can control. The future is not predicted — it's enacted.

**Application**: When the **Demand Gap** lens (02) finds a market signal
that depends on external forces outside the user's control, flag it as
high-risk. Prefer opportunities where the user can directly act.

## Causation vs Effectuation

| Dimension | Causation (MBA Logic) | Effectuation (Expert Logic) |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Starting point | Goal given | Means given |
| Approach | Find resources to achieve goal | Imagine possible effects from means |
| Risk | Maximize expected returns | Minimize affordable loss |
| Competition | Competitive analysis | Partnerships and co-creation |
| Surprises | Avoid or hedge | Leverage as opportunity |
| Prediction | Forecast-based | Control-based |
| Best for | Existing markets with known structure | New markets with high uncertainty |

## When to Use Effectuation vs Causation

**Effectuation** is superior when:
- The market doesn't exist yet (Knightian Uncertainty)
- You have no track record in the domain
- Capital is limited
- You need to start before full information is available

**Causation** is superior when:
- The market is well-defined with known players
- You have domain expertise and a clear competitive advantage
- Capital is available for calculated investments
- The environment is stable enough for forecasting

## Integration With This Skill

Effectuation directly informs execution realism. The 6 lenses identify
WHAT might work. Effectuation determines HOW to proceed given the user's
actual starting position. When an opportunity survives the anti-bias audit,
apply effectuation logic to the execution path:

1. **Means audit** (bird-in-hand): What does the user already have?
2. **Loss tolerance check** (affordable loss): What can they risk?
3. **Partnership scan** (crazy quilt): Who can they involve?
4. **Contingency plan** (lemonade): What if the data is wrong?
5. **Agency check** (pilot-in-the-plane): Can they act directly?
