# Marketing & Narrative Wisdom — The Psychology of Attention and Persuasion

The deployed layer. Where Merchant Wisdom ensures the offer has positive
margin and the research protocols find the truth, this layer wins the
*attention* that turns truth into revenue. It encodes the psychological

message get seen, felt, and acted on.

## 1. The Open Loop: Never Open With a Statement, Open With a Riddle

The most common opening failure is making a *statement* — a closed, finished
claim the audience can agree-with or switch-off-complete. The skilled
marketer opens with an **open loop** — a question, a gap, a mystery that the
brain cannot close, so it keeps reading to resolve it.

- Weak (statement): "Kayaks are affordable."
- Strong (open loop): "This morning I was walking and saw a kayak on a
  stranger's lawn — and realized it was the one I accidentally lost last
  summer."

The open loop hooks because the brain hates unfinished pattern and *must*
resolve the gap. Your message's job is to **force the resolution the
listener will happily accept** at the end: the answer to the riddle is
your offer.

**Rule of the loop:**

- Opening frames arrive resolved: the hook is the *gap*.
- The gap says "that's odd / I need to know," not "this is a fact."
- The resolution comes at the natural close — never before, never after
  the payoff of attention.

## 2. Visual-First: Show the Object Before the Idea

Before any claim or world-building, lead with the raw **visual** of the
object in context. The kayak video opened on the kayak itself — a bare,
physical image that registers before the narrative begins.

Why this works: a raw visual triggers recognition and curiosity in a single
frame with zero reading work. It grounds the future abstraction in something
concrete. Only *after* the eye has the object does the open loop (the riddle)
land.

**Order:**

```
raw visual of the thing  ->  open-loop riddle  ->  story/world  ->  resolve
```

## The Human Story Becomes the Product

"Red Bull doesn't sell the can; Red Bull sells the story." The product is
the *shelf*; the story is what the buyer actually buys and remembers.

- The physical object is a ticket to a feeling the customer wants.
- The human story (who, why, the struggle, the transformation) is what earns
  emotional rent and word-of-mouth's secondary recall.
- When the story *becomes* the product, the product survives commodity
  pricing because no competitor can clone a narrative they didn't live.

**When you treat your product as a story:** you also stop selling features
and start selling the identity the customer steps into. The shift of the red
bull lesson is that the can is interchangeable; the story never is.

## Emotional Value Is the Product

The premise of business is to provide **emotional value**, not objects.
You are in the emotion business; the thing is only the shell.

- The emotional value is *received*, it is felt — calm, status, belonging,
  relief, safety, making.
- Object value is replacable by any competitor at any price; emotional
  value is defensible because it exists in the buyer, not in the shelf.
- Price is set by emotional position more than by cost-tier. Paid solutions
  and low-price rivals both serve *the emotional gap* if the story is the 
  primary claim on the buyer's felt need.

**Testing an offer for emotional value:** name the single emotion it banks
on. If the name is "I don't know" or the answer is a feature list rather
than a felt state, the offer is a shell.

## Diagnosis Over Defense: Proving Yourself Right Is Not a Business Question

A core mental-mode correction: after decisions, do not act and then set out
to *prove the decision was right*. The key operating question is not "how do
I defend my call?" but

> "how do I know my decision is right?"

- Proving yourself right is a sunk-cost ratchet; it keeps you wedded to a
  position.
- Knowing whether you're right is a feedback question: this feeds the
  Decision Ledger, the pre-mortem, and the disconfirming-evidence protocol
  (see `references/research-protocols.md`).
- The emotion-blind discipline of Fang Yuan Mindset + the merchant's
  measure-and-exit discipline both agree on the disposition to the
  evidence: favour the verdicts that confirm, and react to divergences.

**Mechanic:** after any big bet or offer, explicitly record the owned
prediction and *design* to check it within a timebox. That is knowledge;
it keeps ahead of proving. This complements the skill's Lookback Validation
and Decision Journal points.

## The "Emotional Value" Composition Sheet

Use this three-row template on the messaging step, meaning it sits directly
under the copy you write:

| Slot | Positive ask | Negative trap |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| **Open Loop** | Does my first line open a riddle? | It reads like a resume statement |
| **Visual hook** | Do I show the raw object before the story? | I lead with world-building or jargon |
| **Story = product** | Can the buyer state a feeling, not a feature? | I would sell the can, not the story |
| **Emotional value** | What emotion do they feel, by name? | Answer is a list of features |
| **Knowing right** | How will I confirm I am right, on a date? | I will re-defend it to feel correct |

## How This Composes With the Other Modes

```
Merchant Wisdom      -> margin/velocity gate (is the spread real?)
Fang Yuan Mindset    -> strips bias from the story you prefer
Operator Wisdom      -> adds the lofty operation of scale
Marketing Wisdom     -> shapes attention + emotional value into demand

```

The emotional layer is *not* permission to oversell facts. It changes how
the truth is *framed*, never what the truth is. Emotional value must hunt a
genuine order of customer; persuasive framing that bridges an empty claim is
a short campaign, not a business.
