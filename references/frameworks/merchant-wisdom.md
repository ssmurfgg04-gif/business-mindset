# Merchant Wisdom — The Trader's Operating Ethos

The distilled operating rules of the marketplace merchant: the person who
buys low, sells high, moves inventory, and lives by velocity and margin —
not equity, not "disruption," not a funding round. Where Operator Wisdom
draws from tech founders and industrialists, Merchant Wisdom draws from the
oldest, most cash-disciplined lineage in commerce: the trader, the vendor,
the broker, the arbitrageur.

This is the correction layer. It keeps the skill humble, margins-literate,
and cash-flow-literate even when the idea is "modern and venture-flavored."

## The 10 Trading Laws

<merchant_laws>

### 1. Buy Low, Sell High, Always
The entire business reduces to a price difference. If you cannot state the
buy price and the sell price with a positive margin net of ALL costs, you
have a hobby. Every new venture must be expressible as a buy/sell spread.

### 2. Turnover Is Power
A merchant would rather earn a small margin many times than a huge margin
once. Rapid turnover (high velocity) compounds the spread faster than a fat
but slow margin. Velocity × margin = wealth. Optimize velocity per unit of
time and capital, not margin per unit alone.

### 3. Cash Is Blood
The merchant never confuses profit with cash. A sale that sits unpaid in
accounts receivable is not money. Run the business on cash coming in vs.
cash going out. A profitable-but-cashless enterprise dies. Never trust a
holding you cannot convert to cash quickly (the "sell-ability test").

### 4. The First Markup Buys You Nothing
The buyer's first bid is a probe. So is the seller's first ask. Neither is
the honest price. Courtship masks the true spread; the real price is found
in the second or third exchange. Do not anchor on the opening number
(anti-amalgamation-anchoring discipline).

### 5. Cheap Stock Is Not Cheap If It Doesn't Move
A low price with no turnover is dead stock eating cash and shelf space. Cost
doesn't matter if you cannot liquidate it. "Cheap" is meaningless;
"liquid and profitable" is real. Discount to move, because a stale unit
costs money every day it sits.

### 6. Location Matters More Than Honesty of Goods
For the app- and marketplace-arbitrageur, "location" = distribution +
traffic + where the buyer is. A marginally worse product in the right
channel beats a great product nobody sees. Distribution is the prime
location (cross-reference Lens 05 and the operator rule *Distribution >
Product*).

### 7. Borrow the Money of the Lazy
The merchant borrows (working capital, financing, supplier terms, platform
float) not from the eager but from the idle — money that sits should be
put to work. Use other people's idle money and idle time. Who has capital
not yet deployed? Whose capacity sits empty? Pair their slack with your
product.

### 8. Trade Where You Can Measure
The merchant loves the visible quote and the completed trade over the theory.
Markets where price, volume, and margin can be watched (real prices on
marketplaces, actual listing counts, paywalled real cost data) beat retail
markets that hide them. If you cannot see the number, you cannot trade it.

### 9. Shell Out the Risk to Many
Never stake the whole shop on one cargo, one listing, one buyer, one
channel. Spread the bet across SKUs, geographies, and channels like a
vessel's cargo divided among hulls. Survival comes from width of positions,
not depth of a single one.

### 10. Know When There Is No Trade
The merchant reads the true; the reserved and honest operator reads the
market. When the spread closes, when parity hits, when the trade is done.
Walking away is a disciplined ending, not a loss. Hoarding a non-liquid
position because "it must go up" is the trader's death trap.

## The Merchant's Decision Template

For every opportunity, answer as a merchant before any VC-style build:

| Question | What it exposes |
|----------|-----------------|
| What do I buy (from whom, at what price/cost)? | Inputs & supply |
| What do I sell (to whom, at what price)? | Output & demand |
| Inventory cycle: how fast does it turn? | Velocity |
| What ties up capital, and can I liquidate it? | Cash & sell-ability |
| What is the spread after ALL costs & fees? | True margin |
| Where is the buyer (distribution/location)? | Reach |
| What kills my position? | Risk of ruin |

If the merchant template yields no positive spread, the idea is not an
opportunity to the skill regardless of "market size" or "vision."
