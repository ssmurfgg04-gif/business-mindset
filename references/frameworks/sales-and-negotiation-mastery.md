# Sales & Negotiation Mastery — The Money-Capture Layer

Every opportunity, no matter how well analyzed, must eventually be sold. This
framework is the elite layer that converts opportunity into revenue. It deepens
Lens 14 (`references/lenses/14-negotiation-sales.md`) and Lens 09 (pricing) with
the psychology and mechanics the top 1% use to capture the value they create.

Load this framework whenever the output includes a sales motion, a negotiation,
a pricing decision, or a go-to-market step that involves convincing anyone to
pay anything.

---

## 1. The Sales Psychology Stack (Cialdini Applied)

People do not buy features; they buy feelings, status, and avoided pain. The
elite engineer the decision environment.

- **Reciprocity:** give first — genuinely useful insight, a small tool, a
  diagnostic. People repay value with attention and eventually with money.
- **Social proof:** "who else is doing this?" is the buyer's first question.
  Surface testimonials, logos, counts, and case numbers early. Empty proof =
  empty pipeline.
- **Authority:** demonstrate command of the domain. Specifics, numbers, and
  "here's the mechanism" language signal competence better than adjectives.
- **Scarcity (real only):** genuine scarcity (limited capacity, deadline for a
  reason) accelerates decisions. Manufactured scarcity is detected and destroys
  trust — the elite never fake it.
- **Commitment & consistency:** get small yeses first (a meeting, a diagnosis,
  a trial). People who commit in small steps find it hard to say no later.
- **Loss aversion beats gain framing:** "you're losing $X/month without this"
  outsells "you'll gain $X/month with this" roughly 2:1. Frame around the pain
  avoided.

## 2. Outcome-Based Selling — Sell the Transformation, Not the Feature

The feature is the vehicle; the outcome is the product.

- **The transformation map:** where is the customer today (pain, cost, risk),
  and where do they go with your solution (state, revenue, safety)? Sell the
  gap, not the tool.
- **Value-based pricing (from Lens 09):** if your solution saves a customer
  $50K/year, charging $5K captures only 10% of value — often the right capture
  ratio, but never anchor on cost-plus. Anchor on value delivered.
- **The 5-part diagnosis:** before proposing, diagnose: (1) what happens if
  they do nothing, (2) what it's costing them monthly, (3) what they've already
  tried, (4) what "fixed" looks like, (5) what they'd pay for it. This is the
  conversation that closes, not the pitch.
- **Features → proof → outcome:** every feature claim gets a proof and an
  outcome. "Batch export (feature) means your team saves 6 hours a week
  (outcome)."
- **Speak the buyer's language, not yours:** match their vocabulary, their
  industry, their fears. See `top-1-percent-psychology.md` (Ruthless Empathy).

## 3. The Negotiation Ladder

Built on the foundation in `mindsets/elite-cognitive-weapons.md` (BATNA,
anchoring, patience). This is the ladder to the close.

- **Rung 1 — Prepare harder than they do:** know your BATNA, their alternatives,
  the objective market range, and their likely pressure points. Preparation is
  80% of negotiation; the meeting is just execution.
- **Rung 2 — Anchor high, but justifiable:** your first number sets the frame.
  Anchor at the top of the defensible range, never at the middle. Middle
  anchors leave money on the table by construction.
- **Rung 3 — Never split the difference:** splitting rewards the weaker
  position. Instead, "I can't do that number, but here's what I can do" —
  trade concessions for something real, never give them away.
- **Rung 4 — Empathy + firmness (never split the difference method):** label
  their position ("sounds like price is the blocker for you") to defuse it,
  then stay firm on the objective criteria. Be warm on the person, hard on
  the number.
- **Rung 5 — Silence is a tool:** after you make an offer or counter, stop
  talking. The first person to fill the silence loses. Let their words reveal
  their position.
- **Rung 6 — Walk-away power:** the single strongest position in any
  negotiation is a demonstrated willingness to walk. If you cannot walk, you
  are not negotiating — you are begging.

## 4. Objection Engineering — Pre-empt, Don't React

The amateur reacts to objections. The elite make the objections irrelevant
before they are spoken.

- **The 5 universal objections and their counters:**
  1. **Price ("too expensive")** → reframe to cost of inaction and value
     capture ratio. "Compared to the $50K it's saving you, this is 10%."
  2. **Time ("not now")** → isolate the real blocker. "If timing weren't an
     issue, would you move forward?" The answer reveals the true objection.
  3. **Trust ("who are you")** → proof layer: testimonials, case numbers,
     references, and a low-risk entry point (trial, guarantee).
  4. **Inertia ("we're fine")** → diagnose the hidden cost of "fine." Every
     "fine" has a workaround they're paying for somewhere.
  5. **Risk ("what if it fails")** → outcome guarantee, pilot, or step-down
     commitment. Shrink the first step until risk feels acceptable.
- **Pre-empt the top 3:** before they raise it, address it. "You might be
  wondering about X — here's the answer." It builds authority and removes
  friction.
- **Never argue; always diagnose.** An objection is a symptom of an unmet
  need. Find the need, and the objection dissolves.

## 5. Closing Frames — Get the Money Decision

A deal that ends "let me think about it" is a deal that has not been closed.

- **Always ask for the decision explicitly:** the close is a question, not a
  hope. "Should I send over the agreement for $X/month starting Monday?"
- **The right-frame close:** give them a choice between two paths that both
  lead to yes ("do you want to start this week or next?") instead of
  yes/no ("do you want to buy?").
- **Urgency without lying:** deadlines must be real (capacity, price expiry,
  onboarding calendar). False urgency kills long-term trust.
- **Handle "let me think":** "Totally — what specifically would you like to
  think about?" (isolate), then resolve that ONE thing. Unspecified delay is
  a polite no; make it specific.
- **The decision journal for sellers (from `decision-journal.md`):** before
  every significant close, predict the outcome, confidence, and objections
  expected. Calibration makes you a better seller or a failed one — either way,
  it pays to measure.

## 6. The Elite Sales Cadence

- **Pipeline over single deals:** a pipeline of 10 conversations beats
  obsessing over 1. Velocity of outreach → velocity of learning → velocity of
  revenue (see `money-making-mastery.md`, Money Velocity Engine).
- **Follow-up is the sales job:** 80% of sales happen after the 5th contact.
  The amateur quits at 2. Build a cadence (day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30) with a value
  add per touch — never a naked "just checking in."
- **Ask for referrals at the close:** "who else has this problem?" converts
  one win into an addressable market. The best deals are referred deals.
- **Review every lost deal:** "what would have changed your decision?" is the
  cheapest market research you will ever buy. Log it. Feed it to the lens.

## Integration Rules

- **For any output containing a sales step:** include the transformation map
  (current → target state) and the top 3 pre-empted objections.
- **For any negotiation advice:** state the BATNA first, then the anchor, then
  the walk-away threshold. A negotiation plan without a BATNA is a hope.
- **For any pricing advice:** default to value-based over cost-plus, and verify
  the capture ratio (see Lens 09).
- **For any pipeline/growth advice:** emphasize velocity and follow-up cadence
  over "perfect" outreach.
- **Never recommend manipulative or fake-scarcity tactics.** Elite sales
  engineering is honest persuasion; deception is a ToS/trust violation and is
  immediately rejected per the anti-bias gate.
