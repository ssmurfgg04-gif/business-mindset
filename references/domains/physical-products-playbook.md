# Domain Playbook: Physical Products

How the business-mindset lenses adapt for physical products — e-commerce,
manufacturing, hardware, consumer packaged goods (CPG), and other
inventory-based businesses.

## Domain Characteristics

- **Marginal cost**: High — every unit requires materials, manufacturing,
  warehousing, shipping
- **Distribution**: Multi-channel (DTC, retail, wholesale, marketplace)
- **Capital requirement**: Moderate to high — inventory must be funded
  upfront
- **Speed advantage**: Low — manufacturing lead times, shipping times
  constrain iteration speed
- **Regulation**: Significant — product safety, labeling, import/export,
  food/drug (if applicable)
- **Network effects**: Rare; brand and distribution are the moats
- **Leverage**: Capital leverage (inventory) is primary; labor leverage
  (fulfillment); code leverage (DTC site) is secondary

## The Core Challenge

Physical products are the hardest domain for asymmetric execution. Every
unit costs money to make, store, and ship. Inventory ties up capital.
Manufacturing lead times slow iteration. Returns and defects eat margin.

The skill's bias toward permissionless leverage (Lens 04) and zero
marginal cost (Lens 07 Signal 4) will score most physical product
opportunities as Tier 3 (Linear). This is **usually correct**.

Physical products can still be good businesses, but they require
different thinking:
- Brand is the moat ( Lens 06 Moat Check should weight brand heavily)
- Distribution is the leverage (Lens 05 is critical)
- Capital efficiency matters more than upside (Lens 08 Risk of Ruin
  is critical)

## Lens Adaptations

### Lens 01 (Signal Scan) — Physical product specific signals
- **Price increases in a category** (demand exceeds supply)
- **Stockout patterns** (popular products consistently out of stock)
- **Import data shifts** (tariffs, supply chain disruption)
- **Reddit/forum complaints about quality** in a category
- **Instagram/TikTok product virality** (early signal of demand)
- **Retail category growth** (NPD/Circana data if accessible)

### Lens 02 (Demand Gap) — Physical product specific gaps
- **"I wish [product] existed with [feature]"** — feature gap
- **"All [product category] is made in China and quality varies"** — quality gap
- **"I can't find [product] for [specific use case]"** — niche gap
- **"The only [product] options are either cheap-bad or expensive-luxury"** — price-tier gap
- **"I make my own [product] because nothing on the market works"** — DIY signal

### Lens 03 (Arbitrage) — Physical product specific arbitrages
- **Geographic arbitrage**: Source from low-cost region, sell in high-price region
- **Temporal arbitrage**: Buy off-season inventory, sell in-season
- **Platform arbitrage**: Cross-marketplace price differences (Amazon vs eBay vs Etsy)
- **Regulatory arbitrage**: Products legal in one jurisdiction but restricted in another
- **Brand arbitrage**: Commodity product with strong brand commands 3-10x premium

### Lens 04 (Leverage) — Physical product specific leverage
- **Capital leverage is primary** — inventory must be funded
- **Code leverage**: DTC e-commerce site (Shopify), but this is table stakes, not a moat
- **Media leverage**: Brand-building content (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube)
- **Network leverage**: Rare in physical products; possible with marketplaces
- **Labor leverage**: Fulfillment (often outsourced to 3PL)

### Lens 05 (Network Path) — Physical product specific distribution
- **DTC e-commerce** (Shopify) — primary for new brands
- **Amazon** — high reach, but platform dependency (rented distribution)
- **Retail/wholesale** — high capital requirement, slow, but durable
- **Marketplace (Etsy)** — niche, but platform dependency
- **Social commerce** (TikTok Shop, Instagram) — emerging, volatile
- **Pop-up/IFTF/events** — slow, but builds direct relationships

### Lens 06 (Anti-Bias) — Physical product specific bias
- **"I'll manufacture in China and it'll be cheap"** bias: Quality control,
  shipping, tariffs, and lead times eat the savings. Often more expensive
  than domestic manufacturing for small runs.
- **"Amazon will distribution-solve it"** bias: Amazon is rented
  distribution. Algorithm changes, suspensions, and copycats are constant
  risks.
- **"I'll figure out fulfillment later"** bias: Fulfillment is 20-40% of
  the unit economics. Design for it from day 1.
- **"DTC means I keep all the margin"** bias: DTC CAC is often higher
  than wholesale margin sacrifice.

### Lens 07 (Exponential Potential) — Physical product tier expectations
- **Tier 1 (Moonshot)**: Essentially impossible for pure physical
  products. The only path: brand so strong it becomes a platform
  (Patagonia, Supreme — rare, multi-decade builds).
- **Tier 2 (Scalable Linear)**: The realistic target. Brand + efficient
  supply chain + multi-channel distribution. Examples: Allbirds,
  Gymshark, many successful DTC brands.
- **Tier 3 (Linear)**: The default. Commodity products without brand
  moat. Compete on price. Avoid unless you have a specific cost advantage.

### Lens 08 (Risk of Ruin) — Physical product specific risk
- **Financial risk**: HIGH. Inventory is a upfront capital commitment
  that can become worthless if it doesn't sell.
- **Time risk**: Manufacturing lead times (8-16 weeks for overseas)
  mean you commit capital months before you know if the product will sell.
- **Reputation risk**: Product defects, safety issues, returns all
  damage brand directly.
- **Liability risk**: Product liability insurance is non-negotiable.
  Defective products can sue you out of business.

## Industry-Specific Bias Patterns

### Bias 1: "Manufacturing overseas is always cheaper"
For small runs (<1000 units), domestic manufacturing is often cheaper
when you account for shipping, tariffs, quality control trips, and
payment terms. Overseas only wins at scale.

**Counter**: Get quotes from both. Factor in total landed cost, not
just unit cost.

### Bias 2: "I'll start on Amazon, then expand to DTC"
Amazon-first brands struggle to transition to DTC because they never
built an audience. Amazon owns the customer relationship.

**Counter**: Start DTC. Use Amazon as a secondary channel once you
have brand recognition.

### Bias 3: "My product is so good it'll go viral"
Virality is not a strategy. Most "viral" products had 2-5 years of
consistent brand-building before the viral moment.

**Counter**: Lens 05 (Network Path) — what's the sustained distribution
plan? Virality is upside, not plan.

### Bias 4: "I'll raise prices once I have scale"
The opposite is true. Price establishes brand position. Discounting
later is easy; raising prices is hard.

**Counter**: Price at the brand position you want, not the cost-plus
price. If that's not viable, the product isn't viable.

### Bias 5: "Inventory is an asset"
Inventory is a liability until it's sold. It ties up capital, takes
space, and depreciates. Lean inventory beats fat inventory.

**Counter**: Lens 08 (Risk of Ruin) — model the scenario where inventory
doesn't sell. Can you survive it?

## Regulatory Considerations

- **Product safety**: CPSC (US), CE (EU), various national standards.
  Mandatory for toys, electronics, children's products, food-contact.
- **Labeling**: FTC labeling rules, country-of-origin, materials disclosure.
- **Food/drug**: FDA (US) if edible or cosmetic. Significant compliance.
- **Import/export**: Tariffs, customs, product restrictions by country.
- **Insurance**: Product liability insurance is non-negotiable. $1M+
  coverage minimum.
- **Environmental**: Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in EU for
  packaging, electronics, batteries.

## The Brand Moat

For physical products, brand is often the only durable moat. Lens 06
(Moat Check) should weight brand heavily:

- **Brand as moat**: Customers buy because they recognize and trust
  the brand, not because of features or price.
- **Brand building takes time**: 2-5 years minimum for durable brand
  equity. Lens 08 should account for this time investment.
- **Brand enables premium pricing**: 2-5x commodity prices for
  established brands.
- **Brand is non-linear**: Once established, brand compounds (each
  customer acquisition reinforces the brand, lowering future CAC).

The implication: physical product opportunities should be evaluated
on brand potential, not just product quality. A mediocre product
with great brand potential beats a great product with no brand path.

## Case Study Examples

### Success: Allbirds
- **Opportunity**: Sustainable, comfortable everyday shoes
- **Wedge**: Merino wool material (differentiated, story-able)
- **Leverage**: Capital (inventory) + media (sustainability story)
- **Distribution**: DTC-first, then retail expansion
- **Tier**: 2 (Scalable Linear) — brand moat, multi-channel, but
  still physical product economics
- **Lesson**: Brand + material differentiation + DTC-first is a
  proven Tier 2 physical product playbook

### Success: Gymshark
- **Opportunity**: Fitness apparel for serious lifters
- **Wedge**: Influencer marketing before it was saturated
- **Leverage**: Capital + media (influencer content)
- **Distribution**: DTC + influencer network
- **Tier**: 2 (Scalable Linear) — brand moat, but apparel economics
- **Lesson**: Niche audience + influencer distribution can build a
  billion-dollar brand without retail

### Failure: Many DTC brands that over-raised
- **Pattern**: DTC brand raises $50M+ at high valuation. Spends on
  paid acquisition. CAC rises. Margin compresses. Cannot raise again.
  Forced to sell at low valuation or shut down.
- **Why**: Treated paid acquisition as sustainable distribution.
  Didn't build brand moat. Burned capital on growth, not durability.
- **Lesson**: Lens 08 (Risk of Ruin) — if your business requires
  constant capital injection to grow, it's not a business, it's a
  Ponzi scheme with extra steps.

### Failure: Quirky
- **Opportunity**: Crowdsourced invention platform
- **Why failed**: No brand moat, no distribution advantage, products
  were mediocre, unit economics didn't work. Raised $185M, bankrupt
  in 6 years.
- **Lesson**: "Innovation platform" without brand or distribution
  is a zero-moat play. Lens 06 should have rejected this.

## Lens Modifications for Physical Products

| Lens | Modification |
|------|--------------|
| 01 Signal | Price increases and stockouts are primary signals |
| 02 Demand | DIY signals and quality gaps are primary |
| 03 Arbitrage | Geographic and brand arbitrage most common |
| 04 Leverage | Capital leverage primary; brand as media leverage |
| 05 Network | DTC primary; Amazon as secondary (with platform-risk flag) |
| 06 Anti-Bias | Weight brand moat heavily; watch for "overseas is cheaper" bias |
| 07 Exponential | Tier 2 is the realistic ceiling; Tier 1 essentially impossible |
| 08 Risk of Ruin | Inventory risk is the main exposure; model worst-case sell-through |

## Common Anti-Patterns in Physical Products

1. **Over-ordering inventory** — ties up capital, becomes obsolete
2. **Single-source manufacturing** — supply chain disruption kills you
3. **Amazon-first strategy** — never builds brand, platform-dependent
4. **Underpricing for "scale"** — destroys margin without building moat
5. **Ignoring fulfillment costs** — 20-40% of unit economics
6. **Skipping product liability insurance** — one lawsuit ends the business
7. **Treating brand as marketing, not product** — brand is the moat,
  not an expense
8. **Chasing virality** — not a strategy; build durable distribution
9. **Raising too much VC** — forces growth-at-all-costs, destroys
  unit economics discipline
