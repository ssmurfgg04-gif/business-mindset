# Domain Playbook: Services

How the business-mindset lenses adapt for service businesses — consulting,
agencies, freelancing, done-for-you services, and other human-capital-based
businesses.

## Domain Characteristics

- **Marginal cost**: High — your time is the product
- **Distribution**: Often relationship-based, not channel-based
- **Capital requirement**: Very low to start (often $0)
- **Speed advantage**: Variable — fast to start, slow to scale
- **Regulation**: Light for general services; heavy for licensed
  professions (law, medicine, finance, architecture)
- **Network effects**: Rare, but reputation compounds
- **Leverage**: Labor leverage (permissioned) is default; code/media
  leverage possible as a second phase

## The Core Tension

Services businesses are structurally linear (your hours = your revenue).
The skill's bias toward exponential potential (Lens 07) and permissionless
leverage (Lens 04) will often score services as Tier 3 (Linear). This is
**correct** — most services businesses are linear.

But linear is not bad. A profitable services business can:
- Generate immediate cash flow (faster than any SaaS)
- Fund a transition to productized services or SaaS
- Build reputation and network that compound over decades
- Be operated indefinitely with low stress

The skill should not reject services opportunities. It should help the
user understand they're choosing a linear business and structure it
correctly.

## Lens Adaptations

### Lens 01 (Signal Scan) — Services specific signals
- **Hiring difficulty in a niche** (companies can't find [specialist])
- **Consultant rate inflation** (rates rising faster than general market)
- **"We outsource X but it's painful"** complaints
- **Agency acquisition activity** (incumbents buying smaller agencies)
- **New regulation creating compliance service needs**

### Lens 02 (Demand Gap) — Services specific gaps
- **"I hired someone for X and they were terrible"** = quality gap
- **"I can't find anyone who does X for my industry"** = niche gap
- **"The big firms won't take small engagements"** = size gap
- **"Generic consultants don't understand my domain"** = expertise gap

### Lens 03 (Arbitrage) — Services specific arbitrages
- **Skill arbitrage**: Bringing expertise from a high-paying industry to
  a lower-competition niche (e.g., ex-fintech PM doing strategy for
  local credit unions)
- **Geographic arbitrage**: Serving clients in high-rate markets from
  a lower-cost location (remote work enables this)
- **Regulatory arbitrage**: Specialized compliance services for new
  regulations (GDPR, AI Act, etc.)
- **Speed arbitrage**: Faster delivery than incumbents (agencies are
  often slow; a fast solo operator can win)

### Lens 04 (Leverage) — Services specific leverage
- **Labor leverage is the default** — your time, then subcontractors
- **Code leverage** is possible as a second phase: productize the service
  (turn the deliverable into a template/tool/SaaS)
- **Media leverage** works well — content marketing builds authority
  which commands higher rates
- **Network leverage** is rare but possible (marketplace of vetted
  freelancers, community of practitioners)

### Lens 05 (Network Path) — Services specific distribution
- **Direct outreach** is the primary channel (LinkedIn, email, calls)
- **Referrals** become dominant after 12+ months (if quality is high)
- **Content marketing** (blog, LinkedIn, Twitter) builds inbound over time
- **Speaking/podcast appearances** build authority for premium pricing
- **Partnerships** with complementary service providers (dev shop +
  design shop referrals)
- **Avoid marketplace dependence** (Upwork, Fiverr) — race to bottom

### Lens 06 (Anti-Bias) — Services specific bias
- **"I'll scale by hiring"** bias: labor leverage scales linearly.
  Hiring 5 people doesn't 5x your output; it adds management overhead.
- **"I'll productize eventually"** bias: most service businesses never
  productize. Be honest about whether you will.
- **"Premium pricing is greedy"** bias: underpricing is the most common
  services mistake. Charge 2-3x what feels comfortable.

### Lens 07 (Exponential Potential) — Services tier expectations
- **Tier 1 (Moonshot)**: Essentially impossible for pure services.
  Requires transitioning to productized services or SaaS.
- **Tier 2 (Scalable Linear)**: Possible with subcontractors (labor
  leverage) or productized services (code leverage on a service
  deliverable). Revenue scales, but not 100x.
- **Tier 3 (Linear)**: The default. Solo operator trading time for money.
  Fine if intentional; a trap if you expected otherwise.

### Lens 08 (Risk of Ruin) — Services specific risk
- **Financial risk**: Low. Services rarely require capital.
- **Time risk**: High. Every hour invested is non-recoverable.
- **Reputation risk**: Higher than SaaS. Bad client outcomes damage
  your name directly.
- **Burnout risk**: The hidden killer of services businesses.
  Lens 08 should add a burnout check: "Can I sustain this pace for
  24 months without burnout?"

## Industry-Specific Bias Patterns

### Bias 1: "I'll scale by hiring"
Hiring adds management overhead, quality control challenges, and
margin compression. Most service businesses hit a wall at 5-10 employees
because the founder becomes a manager, not a practitioner.

**Counter**: Before hiring, ask: "Can I productize the service first?"
Productized services (fixed scope, fixed price, repeatable process)
scale better than custom services.

### Bias 2: "Hourly billing is fair"
Hourly billing punishes efficiency. If you're good, you should charge
for outcomes, not time.

**Counter**: Move to value-based or project-based pricing as fast as
possible. Hourly is for beginners.

### Bias 3: "I need a website/brand/LLC before I start"
Services businesses start with the first client, not the first deliverable.

**Counter**: Lens 05 says direct outreach is primary. Start there.
Build the website after you have 3 clients.

### Bias 4: "Generalist is safer than specialist"
The opposite is true. Generalists compete on price. Specialists
command premium rates and have less competition.

**Counter**: Pick a niche. Go deep. "Marketing consultant" = $100/hr.
"Marketing consultant for B2B SaaS post-Series A" = $300/hr.

## Regulatory Considerations

- **Licensed professions** (law, medicine, accounting, architecture,
  engineering): Don't practice without the license. Even "consulting"
  can cross the line. Get counsel.
- **Insurance**: Professional liability (E&O) for most services.
  Non-negotiable for high-stakes work.
- **Contracts**: Use real contracts. Verbal agreements lead to disputes.
- **Data handling**: If you see client data, GDPR/CCPA apply. Have a DPA.

## The Productization Path

Many services businesses should plan a transition to productized
services or SaaS. The path:

```
1. Custom services (linear, high margin, low scale)
   ↓ identify the repeatable deliverable
2. Productized services (fixed scope, fixed price, process-driven)
   ↓ identify the software that enables the process
3. SaaS or tool (code leverage, scales without your time)
   ↓ the service becomes onboarding for the SaaS
```

Not every services business should make this transition. But the skill
should ask: "Is productization a goal?" If yes, structure the service
to enable it (document processes, identify repeatable parts, build
internal tools).

## Case Study Examples

### Success: DesignJoy (Brett Williams)
- **Opportunity**: Unlimited design service for a flat monthly fee
- **Wedge**: Async-only, no meetings, fixed scope per request
- **Leverage**: Labor leverage, but productized (process is the leverage)
- **Distribution**: Twitter build-in-public, indie hacker community
- **Tier**: 2 (Scalable Linear) — revenue scales with subscriptions,
  but delivery still requires Brett's time (or subcontractors)
- **Lesson**: Productized services can be Tier 2. The "product" is the
  process, not the deliverable.

### Success: Many niche consultants
- **Opportunity**: Domain expertise in a specific niche
- **Wedge**: "I've done [specific thing] at [specific companies]"
- **Leverage**: Labor leverage + media leverage (content builds authority)
- **Distribution**: LinkedIn, speaking, referrals
- **Tier**: 3 (Linear) — and that's fine. $500K/year consulting is a
  great linear business.
- **Lesson**: Tier 3 is not a failure. Intentional linear is better
  than accidental exponential-chasing.

### Failure: Agencies that tried to scale via hiring
- **Pattern**: 3-person agency grows to 15-person agency. Founder
  becomes manager. Quality drops. Clients leave. Margin compresses.
- **Why**: Labor leverage doesn't compound. Management overhead grows
  faster than capacity.
- **Lesson**: Don't hire to scale. Productize first, then decide if
  hiring makes sense.

## Lens Modifications for Services

| Lens | Modification |
|------|--------------|
| 01 Signal | Hiring difficulty and rate inflation are primary signals |
| 02 Demand | Quality gaps and niche gaps are primary |
| 03 Arbitrage | Skill and speed arbitrage most common |
| 04 Leverage | Labor default; productization as the path to code leverage |
| 05 Network | Direct outreach primary; referrals compound over time |
| 06 Anti-Bias | Watch for "I'll scale by hiring" and "hourly is fair" biases |
| 07 Exponential | Tier 3 is acceptable; Tier 2 requires productization |
| 08 Risk of Ruin | Add burnout check; time is the main exposure |

## Common Anti-Patterns in Services

1. **Competing on price** — race to bottom, destroys margin
2. **Hiring to scale before productizing** — adds overhead without leverage
3. **Generalist positioning** — competes with everyone, premium impossible
4. **Hourly billing past the first year** — punishes efficiency
5. **Ignoring burnout signals** — services businesses kill their operators
6. **Not raising prices annually** — inflation plus experience should
  mean 10-20% annual price increases
7. **Taking every client** — bad clients cost more than they pay
